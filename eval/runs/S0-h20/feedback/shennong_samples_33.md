## Scene 1

> 古代时候，人不知道他们能吃什么植物，常常一吃就死。但是不知道为什么，没有办法。所以有一天，神农出去试一试很多种植物，告诉人什么植物是安全什么是危险。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代时候” / Formula: 在 + time period / Correct: “在古代”

Incorrect phrase: “人不知道他们能吃什么植物” / Formula: 人们 + 不知道 + embedded question; use a clear referent rather than “他们” / Correct: “人们不知道哪些植物可以吃。”

Incorrect phrase: “常常一吃就死” / Formula: subject + 一…就…; for a general possibility, use 会 / Correct: “人们常常一吃就会死。”  
“人” is vague here, so repeating “人们” makes the meaning clearer.

Incorrect phrase: “但是不知道为什么，没有办法” / Formula: subject + 不知道 + why/how; subject + 没有办法 + verb / Correct: “但是人们不知道该怎么办，也没有办法解决这个问题。”  
The original sentence does not clearly say who does not know and what they cannot solve.

Incorrect phrase: “神农出去试一试很多种植物” / Formula: character introduction + 决定/出去 + 试吃 + object / Correct: “于是，神农决定亲自试吃很多种植物。”  
Introduce 神农 as a character when he first appears; “试吃” is the specific verb for trying food or plants.

Incorrect phrase: “告诉人什么植物是安全什么是危险” / Formula: 告诉 + 人们 + 哪些…，哪些… / Correct: “告诉人们哪些植物可以吃，哪些植物有毒。”  

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “常常” / Context: “人们常常一吃就会死。” / Alternatives: “往往” sounds a little more formal and is often used for a repeated result or tendency. “常常” simply means “often” and is very common in everyday speech.

Word: “死” / Context: “一吃就会死。” / Alternatives: “丧命” is more formal and dramatic, often used in historical narratives. “中毒” means “be poisoned,” so it is more precise if the plants do not always cause death.

Word: “试一试” / Context: “试一试很多种植物。” / Alternatives: “试吃” specifically means trying food or edible items, so it fits plants much better. “尝试” is more formal and means “attempt,” but “试吃” is the most natural choice here.

Word: “安全” / Context: “什么植物是安全。” / Alternatives: “无毒” means “non-toxic” and is more precise for plants. “安全” is broader: it can describe places, situations, or objects that are not dangerous.

Word: “危险” / Context: “什么是危险。” / Alternatives: “有毒” directly means “poisonous,” while “有害” means “harmful” and may describe something that causes illness without necessarily being poisonous. Both are more specific than “危险” in this context.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly established the problem first and then showed how Shennong tried to help people, so your story has a logical flow.  
Next time, focus on making the subject clear in every sentence and using specific words such as “试吃” and “有毒.”

## Scene 2

> 发现有的植物对身体非常好，特别是茶，让肚子感觉又暖和又清楚。他告诉朋友：“你吃哪种植物，这就要看你的口味。比方说，你爱吃甜的，可能喜欢糖。”他最喜欢吃花，除了夹竹桃以外，什么都花想吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “发现有的植物对身体非常好” / Formula: Character/name + 发现 + statement / Correct: “神农发现有些植物对身体很有好处。”  
The subject is missing. Introduce the character by name first, so that the later pronoun “他” has a clear referent.

Incorrect phrase: “特别是茶，让肚子感觉又暖和又清楚” / Formula: 特别是 + typical example; 让 + object + 感觉 + adjective / Correct: “特别是茶，能让肚子感觉又暖和又舒服。”  
“清楚” describes thoughts, explanations, or vision, not a physical feeling in the stomach.

Incorrect phrase: “你吃哪种植物，这就要看你的口味。” / Formula: Uncertain question/condition + 这就要看 + A-not-A / wh-question / A还是B + 了 / Correct: “你会不会喜欢一种植物，这就要看你喜欢甜的还是苦的了。”  
With “这就要看……了,” the part after 看 should be a question form, such as “喜欢甜的还是苦的.”

Incorrect phrase: “你爱吃甜的，可能喜欢糖。” / Formula: Subject + 可能 + 喜欢 + 吃 + food / Correct: “你爱吃甜的，可能喜欢吃糖。”  
Use “喜欢吃” when you mean someone enjoys eating a particular food.

Incorrect phrase: “除了夹竹桃以外，什么都花想吃。” / Formula: 除了 + N + 以外，什么 + classifier/noun + 都 + 想 + V / Correct: “除了夹竹桃以外，什么花他都想吃。”  
“什么花” must stay together, and “都” comes before the verb. Adding “他” also makes the subject clear.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “对身体非常好” / Context: “有的植物对身体非常好” / Alternatives: “对身体很有好处” is the most natural everyday expression for something beneficial to health. “有益健康” is more concise and slightly more formal. “非常好” is understandable but broad and less specific.

Word: “暖和” / Context: “让肚子感觉又暖和又舒服” / Alternatives: “温暖” sounds gentler and more descriptive, especially in writing. “暖和” is common in everyday speech, often for weather, clothes, or rooms; it also works for a warm physical feeling.

Word: “口味” / Context: “这就要看你的口味了” / Alternatives: “饮食偏好” means someone’s food preferences and is more formal. “喜好” means general likes and dislikes, so it can be used beyond food. “口味” usually refers to preferred flavours, such as sweet, spicy, or salty.

Word: “爱吃” / Context: “你爱吃甜的” / Alternatives: “喜欢吃” is neutral and very common. “偏爱” means to have a particular preference for something and sounds more advanced: “你偏爱甜食。” “爱吃” is natural but more casual and can imply liking something a lot.

Word: “最喜欢” / Context: “他最喜欢吃花” / Alternatives: “偏爱” is more concise and slightly more literary: “他偏爱吃花。” “钟爱” expresses a strong, special affection and is more formal or expressive. “最喜欢” is clear and natural, especially for beginner-level writing.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well using “特别是” to give tea as a clear example of a beneficial plant, and your sentences have a logical flow from health benefits to food preferences.  
Next time, focus on giving each sentence a clear subject and checking word order in patterns such as “什么花都想吃.”

## Scene 3

> 他发现这个口味，一百岁时候，不小心吃了毒的花。一吃就开始死。但是生病时候告诉人什么植物是药，什么植物是毒。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “他发现这个口味” / Formula: Subject + 发现/尝出 + object; use “味道” for the taste of food or plants / Correct: “他尝出了这种花的味道。”  
“发现这个口味” is unclear here. If you mean he noticed the flower’s taste, “尝出” is more natural.

Incorrect phrase: “一百岁时候” / Formula: 在 + time + 的时候 / Correct: “在一百岁的时候”  
You can also say “他一百岁的时候.”

Incorrect phrase: “毒的花” / Formula: 有毒的 + noun / Correct: “有毒的花”  
“有毒的花” means “a poisonous flower.”

Incorrect phrase: “一吃就开始死” / Formula: Subject + 一 + verb + 就 + result / Correct: “他一吃就快要死了。”  
“开始死” is not natural in Chinese. “快要死了” means “was about to die.”

Incorrect phrase: “但是生病时候告诉人什么植物是药，什么植物是毒” / Formula: 虽然 + situation, 但是 + subject + verb + object / Correct: “虽然他中毒了，但是他告诉人们什么植物是药，什么植物是毒。”  
Use “中毒” when someone becomes ill because they ate something poisonous. Also, “告诉人们” is more natural than “告诉人.”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “发现” / Context: “他发现这个口味” / Alternatives: “尝出” or “察觉到”  
“发现” means “to discover” and is quite general. “尝出” specifically means to detect a taste by eating or drinking, so it fits this scene best. “察觉到” means to notice something subtle, such as a strange taste or a change in one’s body.

Word: “口味” / Context: “这个口味” / Alternatives: “味道” or “滋味”  
“口味” often refers to someone’s preference, such as “我的口味很清淡” (“I prefer light flavors”). “味道” is the usual word for the flavor of a specific flower, food, or medicine. “滋味” can sound more literary and may describe both a physical taste and a life experience.

Word: “毒的花” / Context: “吃了毒的花” / Alternatives: “有毒的花” or “毒花”  
“毒的花” is understandable but incomplete. “有毒的花” is the standard, neutral expression. “毒花” is shorter and more literary, but it is less common in everyday speech.

Word: “生病” / Context: “生病时候” / Alternatives: “中毒” or “身体不舒服”  
“生病” is a general word for being ill. “中毒” is much more precise when the illness is caused by poisonous food or plants. “身体不舒服” is softer and more general; it means “to feel unwell.”

Word: “死” / Context: “开始死” / Alternatives: “快要死了” or “生命垂危”  
“死” is direct and strong. “快要死了” naturally describes someone being close to death. “生命垂危” is more advanced and formal; it means that someone’s life is in critical danger.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly communicated the dramatic cause-and-effect sequence: he ate a poisonous flower, became very ill, and still shared his knowledge with others.  
Next time, focus on using **有毒**, **中毒**, and **一……就……** to make the details more natural and precise.
