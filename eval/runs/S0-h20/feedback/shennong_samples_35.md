## Scene 1

> 在古代，很多医生不知道怎么用药。所以他们找到了很多植物对药。神农先看到很多病人，他们吃很多植物对药。但是我觉得他们的吃的植物，让他们病人。神农觉得这个植物可能特别是毒。所以他想找一个植物对药，他们可以吃对药。正好，他找到了一把花，他想把花放在茶里，对药。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “找到了很多植物对药” / Formula: 找到 + [object] + 用来/可以 + [purpose] / Correct: “找到了很多可以用来做药的植物。”

Incorrect phrase: “他们吃很多植物对药” / Formula: Subject + 吃了 + [object] + 当药 / Correct: “他们吃了很多植物当药。”

Incorrect phrase: “但是我觉得他们的吃的植物，让他们病人” / Formula: Subject + 觉得 + [noun phrase] + 让 + someone + 生病 / Correct: “但是神农觉得他们吃的植物让他们生病了。”  
“我觉得” unexpectedly changes the narrator to “I,” and “让他们病人” should be “让他们生病.”

Incorrect phrase: “这个植物可能特别是毒” / Formula: [Thing] + 可能 + 有毒 / Correct: “这些植物可能有毒。”  
Use “这些植物” because the previous sentence discusses many plants.

Incorrect phrase: “所以他想找一个植物对药，他们可以吃对药” / Formula: 所以 + Subject + 想找 + [object] + 用来 + [purpose] / Correct: “所以他想找一种可以用来治病的植物，让病人吃。”  
“一种植物” is more natural than “一个植物,” and “对药” is not a correct expression.

Incorrect phrase: “正好，他找到了一把花，他想把花放在茶里，对药” / Formula: 正好 + Subject + 找到 + [object]；想把 + object + 放进 + place + 里 + 用来 + [purpose] / Correct: “正好，他找到了一朵花，想把花放进茶里，用来治病。”  
Use “一朵花” for one flower; “一把花” usually means a bunch of flowers.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “医生” / Context: “很多医生不知道怎么用药” / Alternatives: “医者” or “大夫.” “医生” is the standard modern word for a doctor. “医者” sounds more literary and suits an ancient story, while “大夫” is a common traditional term for a doctor.

Word: “很多” / Context: “很多医生”“很多植物” / Alternatives: “许多” or “各种.” “很多” is very common and natural, but repeating it makes the writing less varied. “许多” is slightly more formal, while “各种植物” emphasizes many different kinds of plants.

Word: “用药” / Context: “不知道怎么用药” / Alternatives: “治病” or “配药.” “用药” means using medicine, often in a medical-treatment context. “治病” clearly means treating illness, while “配药” means preparing or combining medicinal ingredients.

Word: “有毒” / Context: “这些植物可能有毒” / Alternatives: “有害” or “带有毒性.” “有毒” directly means poisonous. “有害” is broader and means harmful, while “带有毒性” is more formal and scientific.

Word: “放在茶里” / Context: “把花放进茶里” / Alternatives: “泡茶” or “煮成药茶.” “放进茶里” simply describes putting something into tea. “泡茶” means steeping it in hot water to make tea, and “煮成药茶” means boiling it into medicinal tea.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well using a clear cause-and-effect storyline: doctors try plants, people get sick, and Shennong looks for a solution.  
Next time, focus on using “当药” or “用来治病” instead of “对药,” and keep the subject consistent throughout the story.

## Scene 2

> 他不知道如果这把花对你的身体好，这就要看如果这把花毒不毒了。神农想找别的植物，但是这很麻烦。正好，他找到了别的花。他一吃花，很快就生病了。他的肚子疼死了。神农不知道他吃不吃灵芝或者夹竹桃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “他不知道如果这把花对你的身体好” / Formula: Subject + 不知道 + embedded yes/no question (A-not-A), and keep the person reference consistent / Correct: “神农不知道这束花对自己的身体好不好。”

Incorrect phrase: “这就要看如果这把花毒不毒了” / Formula: (Uncertain result)，这就要看 + question form (A-not-A / wh-word / A还是B) + 了 / Correct: “这束花对身体有没有好处，这就要看它有没有毒了。”

Incorrect phrase: “但是这很麻烦” / Formula: When referring to a previous action, name the action clearly: 可是/但是 + action + 很 + adjective / Correct: “可是，找别的植物很麻烦。”

Incorrect phrase: “正好，他找到了别的花” / Formula: Use a time/sequence connector when an event happens later: 后来/最后/终于 + subject + verb / Correct: “后来，他找到了另一种花。”  
“正好” means “just at the right moment” or “exactly,” so it is less suitable here.

Incorrect phrase: “他一吃花，很快就生病了” / Formula: Subject + 一 + verb + specific object, 就 + result / Correct: “他一吃这种花，就很快生病了。”  
“花” is too general here; “这种花” clearly refers to the flower he found.

Incorrect phrase: “他的肚子疼死了” / Formula: Subject + 疼得 + degree/result complement / Correct: “他的肚子疼得要命。”  
“疼死了” can be casual exaggeration, but it sounds confusing because Shennong continues doing things afterward.

Incorrect phrase: “神农不知道他吃不吃灵芝或者夹竹桃” / Formula: Subject + 不知道 + 自己 + 应该 + A 还是 B / Correct: “神农不知道自己该吃灵芝还是夹竹桃。”  
Use “自己” because “他” could refer to someone else. Also, “A还是B” is the natural form for choosing between two things.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “这把花” / Context: “这把花对你的身体好” / Alternatives: “这束花” means “a bunch/bouquet of flowers” and is the usual classifier for flowers. “这把花” is understandable but less natural in this context.

Word: “别的植物” / Context: “神农想找别的植物” / Alternatives: “其他植物” is slightly more formal and is common in writing. “别的植物” is natural in everyday speech; “其他植物” makes the narration sound smoother.

Word: “很麻烦” / Context: “找别的植物很麻烦” / Alternatives: “很不容易” emphasizes that something is difficult to achieve. “很费劲” emphasizes that it requires a lot of effort. “很麻烦” is broad and can mean inconvenient or troublesome.

Word: “正好” / Context: “正好，他找到了别的花” / Alternatives: “后来” simply marks the next event in the story. “终于” emphasizes that he found the flower after effort or waiting. Both fit the story better than “正好.”

Word: “生病” / Context: “很快就生病了” / Alternatives: “中毒了” specifically means “was poisoned,” which fits a story about potentially poisonous flowers. “感到不舒服” means “felt unwell” and is softer and less specific than “生病.”

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well by creating a clear cause-and-effect story: Shennong finds a flower, eats it, and then feels ill.  
Next time, focus on using **A-not-A questions** after “不知道” and “这就要看,” and make pronoun references like “他” and “你” consistent.

## Scene 3

> 别的人给了神农一个灵芝。这个植物可以让神农更好。比方说，他会觉得更健康。吃灵芝以后，他可以再走。现在他觉得更好。最后，神农找到了很多植物，他可以用对药。这些植物让神农很高兴。神农想跟很多人分享他的新药。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “这个植物可以让神农更好” / Formula: 让 + 人 + 身体/状态 + 更 + adjective / Correct: “这种植物可以让神农的身体更好。”  
“更好” needs a clearer reference, such as his body or health.

Incorrect phrase: “比方说，他会觉得更健康” / Formula: 也就是说，+ explanation; or 吃了……以后，+ result / Correct: “也就是说，吃了灵芝以后，神农觉得更健康。”  
“比方说” introduces an example, but this sentence explains the result instead of giving an example.

Incorrect phrase: “吃灵芝以后，他可以再走” / Formula: 吃了……以后，+ subject + 可以/能 + 继续 + verb / Correct: “吃了灵芝以后，神农可以继续赶路。”  
“再走” can sound unclear here. “继续赶路” clearly means that he can continue his journey.

Incorrect phrase: “他可以用对药” / Formula: 用 + object + 做/作为 + noun; 把 + object + 用作 + noun / Correct: “他可以用这些植物做药。”  
You need to state what he uses. “用对药” is not a natural verb-object combination.

Incorrect phrase: “神农想跟很多人分享他的新药” / Formula: 把 + object + 分享给 + person / Correct: “神农想把他新发现的药材分享给很多人。”  
This structure makes the thing being shared and the people receiving it clearer.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “别的人” / Context: “别的人给了神农一个灵芝。” / Alternatives: “有人” or “一个人.” “别人” usually means “other people” in contrast to a specific person, while “有人” is more natural when introducing an unknown person in a story. For example: “有人给了神农一株灵芝。”

Word: “这个植物” / Context: “这个植物可以让神农更好。” / Alternatives: “这种植物” or “灵芝.” “这个” points to one specific item, whereas “这种植物” refers to a type of plant and sounds more natural when explaining its effects. Since you already named it, simply saying “灵芝” is also clear.

Word: “更好” / Context: “神农现在觉得更好。” / Alternatives: “更健康” or “精神好多了.” “更好” is very general. “更健康” specifically describes improved health, while “精神好多了” means he feels much more energetic and is useful after illness or tiredness.

Word: “走” / Context: “他可以再走。” / Alternatives: “继续赶路” or “继续旅行.” “走” only means to walk or leave, while “继续赶路” emphasizes travelling onward toward a destination. “继续旅行” is broader and focuses on his journey.

Word: “新药” / Context: “分享他的新药。” / Alternatives: “新发现的药材” or “草药.” “新药” often refers to a newly developed modern medicine. “新发现的药材” fits Shennong’s story because he is discovering medicinal plants, and “草药” emphasizes herbal medicine.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You told the story in a clear time order and showed well how lingzhi helps Shennong feel healthier and continue his journey.  
Next time, focus on using more specific words for health, travel, and medicinal plants, and clearly state what an action is applied to.
