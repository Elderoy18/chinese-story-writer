## Scene 1

> 很久以前，古代的人很不知道什么植物能吃，也什么植物不能吃。所以很多人常常吃不安全的植物，吃不安全的植物对你的身体有坏处，很麻烦的情况。但是一个人，神农，要解决这个问题。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的人很不知道什么植物能吃，也什么植物不能吃” / Formula: 人们 + 不知道 + 什么……能……，什么……不能…… / Correct: “古代的人不知道什么植物能吃，什么植物不能吃。”

Incorrect phrase: “所以很多人常常吃不安全的植物” / Formula: 很多人 + 常常 + 吃 + 不安全的植物 / Correct: “所以很多人常常吃不安全的植物。”  
This sentence is grammatically correct, but it would flow more naturally after the first sentence with “因此” or “所以”.

Incorrect phrase: “吃不安全的植物对你的身体有坏处，很麻烦的情况” / Formula: 吃……对 + 人的身体 + 有坏处；这/这种情况 + 很…… / Correct: “吃不安全的植物对人的身体有坏处，这种情况很麻烦。”  
“你的身体” is vague because the sentence is about many people; use “人的身体” or “人们的身体.” Also, “很麻烦的情况” needs a subject such as “这是一种很麻烦的情况.”

Incorrect phrase: “但是一个人，神农，要解决这个问题” / Formula: 但是/后来 + 有一个人叫……，他 + 想要/决定 + 解决…… / Correct: “但是，有一个人叫神农，他想要解决这个问题。”  
When introducing a new character, introduce him clearly first. “要解决” can mean “needs to solve,” while “想要解决” expresses his intention.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “很久以前” / Context: “很久以前，古代的人……” / Alternatives: “在古代” or “远古时期.” “很久以前” is simple and natural for stories. “在古代” directly sets a historical setting, while “远古时期” sounds more historical and literary.

Word: “古代的人” / Context: “古代的人不知道……” / Alternatives: “古人” or “当时的人们.” “古代的人” is understandable but slightly wordy. “古人” is concise and natural; “当时的人们” emphasizes the people living in that particular time.

Word: “不安全的植物” / Context: “吃不安全的植物” / Alternatives: “有毒的植物” or “不能食用的植物.” “不安全的植物” is broad: it could mean poisonous, dirty, or otherwise risky. “有毒的植物” specifically means poisonous plants, while “不能食用的植物” means plants that should not be eaten.

Word: “有坏处” / Context: “对人的身体有坏处” / Alternatives: “有害” or “会危害健康.” “有坏处” is common and beginner-friendly. “有害” is more concise and formal, while “会危害健康” clearly emphasizes damage to health.

Word: “很麻烦的情况” / Context: “很麻烦的情况” / Alternatives: “一个严重的问题” or “令人担忧的问题.” “麻烦” often means inconvenient or troublesome. “严重的问题” shows that the problem has important consequences, while “令人担忧的问题” emphasizes that people should feel concerned about it.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly explained the problem first and then introduced Shennong as someone who will solve it, which gives your story a strong beginning.  
Next time, focus on using complete sentence structures and introducing new characters with “有一个人叫……”.

## Scene 2

> 他又聪明又努力，旅行很远找到很多植物。比方说，灵芝和夹竹桃。他一吃各种各样的植物就知道能不能吃这些植物。然后，神农写他的主意。他喜欢植物的味道，特别是花。神农遇到了一个黄色的花，一吃就觉得不舒服。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “他又聪明又努力” / Formula: First mention = name + brief identity / Correct: “神农是一位又聪明又努力的人。”  
“他” is unclear at the beginning because the reader does not yet know who this person is.

Incorrect phrase: “旅行很远找到很多植物” / Formula: Subject + 为了…… + 去/到……旅行 + 找到…… / Correct: “为了寻找可以吃的植物，神农去了很远的地方旅行，找到了很多植物。”  
Use a purpose phrase and add “去了很远的地方” to make the sentence more natural.

Incorrect phrase: “比方说，灵芝和夹竹桃。” / Formula: 例如/比如 + noun phrase can follow the previous sentence / Correct: “比如灵芝和夹竹桃。”  
“比方说” is possible, but “比如” is more concise here. This should also be connected more clearly to the previous sentence.

Incorrect phrase: “他一吃各种各样的植物就知道能不能吃这些植物。” / Formula: 一 + 动作 + 就 + result; 这就要看 + question form + 了 / Correct: “他尝一种植物，就能知道这种植物能不能吃吗？这就要看他吃了以后有没有不舒服了。”  
The original meaning is too absolute: eating a plant does not automatically mean he knows whether it is edible. “能不能吃” is an uncertain question, so it works naturally after “这就要看……了.”

Incorrect phrase: “然后，神农写他的主意。” / Formula: Subject + 写下/记录 + information / Correct: “然后，神农把他的发现和想法写下来。”  
“写主意” is not natural in Chinese; we usually write down ideas, discoveries, or notes.

Incorrect phrase: “神农遇到了一个黄色的花” / Formula: 遇到/看到 + 一朵 + adjective + 花 / Correct: “神农看到了一朵黄色的花。”  
Use the measure word “朵” for flowers. Also, one normally “sees” a flower rather than “meets” it.

Incorrect phrase: “一吃就觉得不舒服” / Formula: Subject + 一 + 吃 + object + 就 + feel + adjective / Correct: “他一吃这种花，就觉得不舒服。”  
Add “他” and “这种花” so that the subject and object are clear.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “努力” / Context: “他又聪明又努力” / Alternatives: “勤奋” or “刻苦.” “努力” is a broad and common word meaning “to make an effort.” “勤奋” describes someone who works consistently and diligently, while “刻苦” emphasizes working especially hard despite difficulty.

Word: “旅行很远” / Context: “旅行很远找到很多植物” / Alternatives: “远行” or “跋山涉水.” “旅行很远” is understandable but not very natural. “远行” means traveling far away and is concise. “跋山涉水” is more literary and vividly suggests crossing mountains and rivers through hardship.

Word: “各种各样的植物” / Context: “他一吃各种各样的植物……” / Alternatives: “各类植物” or “不同种类的植物.” “各种各样的” is very common and conversational. “各类” is more concise and slightly more formal; “不同种类的” clearly emphasizes variety by type.

Word: “主意” / Context: “神农写他的主意” / Alternatives: “想法,” “发现,” or “记录.” “主意” usually means an idea or plan for what to do. “想法” is a general thought, “发现” emphasizes what he learned through exploration, and “记录” refers to written notes or documentation.

Word: “觉得不舒服” / Context: “一吃就觉得不舒服” / Alternatives: “感到不适” or “出现中毒症状.” “觉得不舒服” is natural in everyday speech. “感到不适” is more formal and concise, while “出现中毒症状” is more specific and dramatic, suitable if the flower is poisonous.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well using a clear sequence of events and including specific plant examples such as 灵芝 and 夹竹桃.  
Next time, focus on introducing the main character clearly and making sure each sentence has an explicit subject and object.

## Scene 3

> 发现了夹竹桃有毒。别人看到他，很快给他吃一个灵芝。神农一吃就觉得比较好。然后，他继续写他发现的信息。会告诉他的朋友哪一个植物能不能吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “发现了夹竹桃有毒。” / Formula: Subject + 发现 + object / Correct: “神农发现夹竹桃有毒。”  
The original sentence is missing the subject, so it is unclear who made the discovery.

Incorrect phrase: “别人看到他，很快给他吃一个灵芝。” / Formula: Specific person + 看到 + person + 后，给 + person + 吃 + object / Correct: “神农的朋友看到他后，很快给他吃了一个灵芝。”  
“别人” is too vague here; use a specific person. Also, add “了” because giving him the lingzhi is a completed action.

Incorrect phrase: “神农一吃就觉得比较好。” / Formula: Subject + 一 + verb + 就 + feeling/result / Correct: “神农一吃灵芝，就觉得好多了。”  
“比较好” normally compares two things or situations. “好多了” naturally means “much better.”

Incorrect phrase: “然后，他继续写他发现的信息。” / Formula: Subject + 继续 + 写下/记录 + object / Correct: “然后，他继续写下他发现的信息。”  
“写下” is more natural when recording information in writing.

Incorrect phrase: “会告诉他的朋友哪一个植物能不能吃。” / Formula: Subject + 会/要 + 告诉 + person + 哪些/什么 + noun + 能不能 + verb / Correct: “他会告诉他的朋友哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃。”  
This sentence needs a subject. Also, “哪一个植物能不能吃” is not natural; use “哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃” to express both possibilities clearly.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “别人” / Context: “别人看到他” / Alternatives: “朋友” or “附近的人.”  
“别人” simply means “other people” and is vague. “朋友” is clearer if Shennong’s friends help him, while “附近的人” means people nearby and suits a scene where someone quickly comes to help.

Word: “比较好” / Context: “神农一吃就觉得比较好” / Alternatives: “好多了” or “舒服多了.”  
“比较好” means “relatively better” and usually needs a comparison. “好多了” means his condition improved significantly; “舒服多了” specifically emphasizes that his body feels much more comfortable.

Word: “写” / Context: “继续写他发现的信息” / Alternatives: “记录” or “写下.”  
“写” is a basic, general word for writing. “记录” means to document or keep a record, so it is especially suitable for scientific discoveries. “写下” emphasizes putting information down in writing.

Word: “信息” / Context: “他发现的信息” / Alternatives: “发现” or “知识.”  
“信息” is general information. “发现” emphasizes the things Shennong discovered, such as which plants are poisonous; “知识” emphasizes useful knowledge that he learned through experience.

Word: “灵芝” / Context: “给他吃一个灵芝” / Alternatives: “灵芝药材” or “草药.”  
“灵芝” is correct and specific. “灵芝药材” highlights that lingzhi is being used as medicinal material, while “草药” is a broader term for herbal medicine.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly described the key story sequence: Shennong discovers the poison, eats lingzhi, recovers, and shares his discoveries.  
Next time, focus on including clear subjects and using specific nouns instead of vague words such as “别人.”
