## Scene 1

> 古代的时候，有很多人不知道什么植物能吃，什么植物不能吃。有很多人吃植物以后就吃坏肚子了，因为他们吃了毒。那个时候，有一个老人叫神农，他什么植物都觉得好吃，不怕生病，不怕毒。他希望别人也可以吃好吃的植物。可是他知道，如果他们还怕吃毒，不会吃他喜欢的植物。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的时候” / Formula: 在 + historical time period / Correct: “在古代”

Incorrect phrase: “吃植物以后就吃坏肚子了” / Formula: 吃了 + food + 以后，+ 就 + 吃坏了 + body part / Correct: “吃了植物以后就吃坏了肚子。”

Incorrect phrase: “因为他们吃了毒” / Formula: 因为 + subject + 吃了 + 有毒的植物 / Correct: “因为他们吃了有毒的植物。”

Incorrect phrase: “不怕毒” / Formula: 不怕 + result/event; or 不怕 + 有毒的 + noun / Correct: “不怕中毒” or “不怕有毒的植物。”

Incorrect phrase: “如果他们还怕吃毒，不会吃他喜欢的植物” / Formula: 如果 + condition，subject + 就 + result; “怕吃毒” → “怕吃有毒的植物” / Correct: “可是他知道，如果他们害怕吃有毒的植物，就不会吃他喜欢的植物。”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “很多人” / Context: “有很多人不知道……” / Alternatives: “人们” is more natural when making a general statement about people in society. “许多人” is slightly more formal and emphasizes a large number. For example: “人们不知道什么植物能吃。”

Word: “吃坏肚子” / Context: “就吃坏了肚子” / Alternatives: “肚子疼” is simple and direct, while “食物中毒” is more precise and serious: it means becoming ill because of unsafe or poisonous food.

Word: “毒” / Context: “吃了毒” / Alternatives: “有毒的植物” clearly identifies the danger as poisonous plants. “毒物” is a more formal word for poisonous substances.

Word: “好吃” / Context: “他什么植物都觉得好吃” / Alternatives: “美味” is more literary and often describes food that tastes excellent. “可口” means tasty and pleasant to eat, with a slightly more refined tone.

Word: “希望” / Context: “他希望别人也可以吃……” / Alternatives: “想让” is more conversational and direct: “他想让别人也能吃……”. “盼望” expresses a stronger, more heartfelt hope.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly explained the problem people faced and introduced Shennong’s motivation in a logical, easy-to-follow way.  
Next time, focus on using more precise expressions for poisoning, such as “有毒的植物” and “中毒.”

## Scene 2

> 所以，他上山下山找植物，吃了很多样子的植物。他是第一人，找到了茶。他把所有的植物写下来了。他一直都吃了，他连毒都能吃。可是他还没吃了一个叫夹竹桃的植物。他找到了那个像星星的植物，觉得很漂亮，马上就吃下了。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “他上山下山找植物，吃了很多样子的植物” / Formula: 吃了 + 很多种 + 名词 / Correct: “他上山下山寻找植物，吃了很多种植物。”

Incorrect phrase: “他是第一人，找到了茶” / Formula: 某人是第一个 + 动词 + 宾语 + 的人 / Correct: “他是第一个发现茶的人。”

Incorrect phrase: “他一直都吃了” / Formula: 一直 + 在 + 动词；or 一直 + 动词 / Correct: “他一直在吃各种植物。”  
“了” does not work here because this describes an ongoing/repeated action.

Incorrect phrase: “他连毒都能吃” / Formula: 连 + 名词短语 + 都 + 动词；completed event: 连……都 + 动词 + 了 / Correct: “他连有毒的植物都吃了。”  
“能吃” emphasizes ability, while the story means that he actually ate poisonous plants.

Incorrect phrase: “可是他还没吃了一个叫夹竹桃的植物” / Formula: 还没（有）+ 动词；or 还没有 + 动词 + 过 / Correct: “可是他还没有吃过一种叫夹竹桃的植物。”  
After “没/没有,” do not use “了.”

Incorrect phrase: “他找到了那个像星星的植物，觉得很漂亮，马上就吃下了” / Formula: 主语 + 把 + 宾语 + 动词 + 结果补语 / Correct: “他找到了一种像星星一样的植物，觉得它很漂亮，马上就把它吃下去了。”  
Adding “它” makes the object clear.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “找” / Context: “上山下山找植物” / Alternatives: “寻找” is more formal and fits a story or historical narrative better. “采集” means to collect plants, so use it when he brings the plants back rather than merely looks for them.

Word: “写下来” / Context: “他把所有的植物写下来了” / Alternatives: “记录下来” is more natural for recording information such as a plant’s name, appearance, and whether it is poisonous. “记载下来” is more literary and is often used for preserving information in written historical records.

Word: “漂亮” / Context: “觉得很漂亮” / Alternatives: “美丽” is a slightly more literary and elegant word for visual beauty. “艳丽” emphasizes bright, striking colors, which is especially suitable for describing flowers such as 夹竹桃.

Word: “马上” / Context: “马上就吃下了” / Alternatives: “立刻” is a more formal word meaning “immediately.” “毫不犹豫地” means “without hesitation” and highlights that he ate the plant too quickly or carelessly.

Word: “很多种植物” / Context: “吃了很多种植物” / Alternatives: “各种植物” means “all kinds of plants” and sounds broad and natural in this context. “不同种类的植物” is more formal and emphasizes that the plants belonged to different categories.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You told the events in a clear chronological order and used “可是” effectively to create suspense before the dangerous plant appears.  
Next time: focus especially on using “没/没有” without “了,” and on making objects clear with “把 + object + verb.”

## Scene 3

> 可是夹竹桃的毒很重，让他肚子很痛。他在地上躺着，觉得他快会死了。可是正好有别人找到他了，给了他一个像伞的植物，叫灵芝。他吃灵芝以后就觉得很好，不再生病了。他测量起来了，像星星的植物是毒，像伞的植物是药。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “夹竹桃的毒很重” / Formula: N. + 很 + adjective; for poison, use 有毒 / 毒性很强 / Correct: “夹竹桃有毒” or “夹竹桃的毒性很强。”

Incorrect phrase: “让他肚子很痛” / Formula: 让 + person + verb phrase; “肚子痛” is the verb phrase / Correct: “让他肚子痛得很厉害。”

Incorrect phrase: “觉得他快会死了” / Formula: subject + 觉得 + 自己/subject + 快要 + verb; avoid an unclear pronoun / Correct: “觉得自己快要死了。”

Incorrect phrase: “正好有别人找到他了” / Formula: someone specific + 找到 + person; avoid vague “别人” / Correct: “正好有一个人找到了他。”

Incorrect phrase: “给了他一个像伞的植物，叫灵芝” / Formula: 给 + person + N.; N. + 叫 + name / Correct: “给了他一种像伞的植物，叫做灵芝。”

Incorrect phrase: “他测量起来了” / Formula: subject + 总结/发现 + information; 测量 means “to measure,” not “to draw a conclusion” / Correct: “他总结出来了：像星星的植物有毒，像伞的植物是药材。”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “毒很重” / Context: “夹竹桃的毒很重” / Alternatives: “毒性很强” is more natural and precise for describing how harmful a poison is. “有剧毒” is stronger and means extremely poisonous. “毒很重” is understandable, but it is less idiomatic.

Word: “肚子很痛” / Context: “让他肚子很痛” / Alternatives: “肚子剧痛” means severe stomach pain and makes the scene more vivid. “腹痛” is a more formal written expression. “肚子很痛” is common and simple.

Word: “在地上躺着” / Context: “他在地上躺着” / Alternatives: “倒在地上” emphasizes that he collapsed, perhaps because he was poisoned. “躺在地上” simply describes his position and does not necessarily imply an emergency.

Word: “觉得很好” / Context: “吃灵芝以后就觉得很好” / Alternatives: “觉得好多了” means he felt much better after being ill, so it fits this context especially well. “恢复了精神” emphasizes regaining energy and strength. “觉得很好” is too general here.

Word: “植物” / Context: “一个像伞的植物” / Alternatives: “药材” emphasizes that 灵芝 is used as medicine. “菌类” is biologically more accurate because 灵芝 is a fungus rather than a plant. “植物” is a broad, everyday term.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly showed the cause-and-effect sequence—poisoning, rescue, recovery, and Shennong’s discovery—and your story has a logical flow.  
Next time, focus on using more precise words for medicine and poisoning, and make sure pronouns clearly refer to the right person.
