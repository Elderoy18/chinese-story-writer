## Scene 1

> 古代的时候，有一个男人，他的名字是神农。他看到了有很多人不知道哪个植物好吃，也不知道什么植物对你的身体有好处。这个问题神农觉得很糟糕，所以神农去了吃每种植物。他吃了一种植物以后，就写了这个植物对你有没有好处。有的时候，神农吃了很好吃的植物。有的时候，他吃了很危险的植物。他吃完了每种植物以后，会来跟他的朋友聊到他吃的植物了。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的时候，有一个男人，他的名字是神农。” / Formula: 在古代，+ character name + 是/是一位 + identity / Correct: “在古代，有一位叫神农的男子。”  
“古代的时候” is understandable, but “在古代” is more natural. This also introduces the character more smoothly.

Incorrect phrase: “他看到了有很多人不知道哪个植物好吃” / Formula: 看到了 + clause / 发现 + clause; 哪种 + noun / Correct: “他发现很多人不知道哪种植物可以吃。”  
Use “发现” when someone realizes a situation. For types of plants, say “哪种植物,” not “哪个植物.” “好吃” means tasty, but the key question here is whether a plant is edible.

Incorrect phrase: “也不知道什么植物对你的身体有好处” / Formula: 什么植物对 + possessive noun + 身体 + 有好处 / Correct: “也不知道什么植物对身体有好处。”  
“你的” is vague here: whose body does it mean? Since you mean people in general, “对身体有好处” is clearer.

Incorrect phrase: “这个问题神农觉得很糟糕” / Formula: 神农觉得 + noun phrase + 很 + adjective / Correct: “神农觉得这个问题很严重。”  
The subject should come before “觉得.” “严重” is more natural than “糟糕” for describing a public health or knowledge problem.

Incorrect phrase: “所以神农去了吃每种植物。” / Formula: 所以 + subject + 去/开始 + verb phrase / Correct: “所以神农开始尝试每一种植物。”  
“去吃” needs a destination or context, and “每种植物” should be “每一种植物.” “尝试” is especially suitable for testing plants.

Incorrect phrase: “他吃了一种植物以后，就写了这个植物对你有没有好处。” / Formula: subject + 尝过/吃了 + noun + 以后，就 + 记录/写下 + information / Correct: “他尝过一种植物以后，就写下这种植物对身体有没有好处。”  
“写了” needs clearer content; “写下/记录” works better. Also, use “这种植物” to refer back to the plant just mentioned, and avoid the vague “你.”

Incorrect phrase: “有的时候，神农吃了很好吃的植物。” / Formula: 有时候 + subject + verb + adjective + 的 + noun / Correct: “有时候，神农尝到很好吃的植物。”  
For a repeated habit, do not use “了.” “尝到” emphasizes that he discovered or experienced the taste.

Incorrect phrase: “有的时候，他吃了很危险的植物。” / Formula: 有时候 + subject + verb + dangerous noun / Correct: “有时候，他也会吃到有毒的植物。”  
Again, remove “了” for a habitual action. Plants are more naturally described as “有毒的” than “危险的.”

Incorrect phrase: “他吃完了每种植物以后，会来跟他的朋友聊到他吃的植物了。” / Formula: subject + 尝完/吃完 + noun + 以后，+ 会/就会 + 跟 + person + 聊/谈论 + topic / Correct: “他尝完每一种植物以后，就会跟朋友聊一聊他尝过的植物。”  
Do not combine completed “吃完了” with habitual “会” in this way. “聊到” is less natural here; use “聊一聊” or “谈论.” The final “了” is unnecessary.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “男人” / Context: “有一个男人，他的名字是神农。” / Alternatives: “男子” or “人物.” “男人” simply means an adult male and is neutral but plain. “男子” sounds more natural in a historical story, while “人物” is useful when introducing an important historical or legendary figure.

Word: “好吃” / Context: “不知道哪个植物好吃。” / Alternatives: “可以吃的” or “可食用的.” “好吃” means delicious, so it focuses on flavor. “可以吃的” means edible and is appropriate for basic writing; “可食用的” is more formal and scientific.

Word: “糟糕” / Context: “这个问题神农觉得很糟糕。” / Alternatives: “严重” or “令人担忧.” “糟糕” is a general, informal word for something bad. “严重” stresses the importance or harmful consequences of a problem, while “令人担忧” means “worrying” and gives the sentence a more thoughtful tone.

Word: “吃每种植物” / Context: “神农去了吃每种植物。” / Alternatives: “尝试每一种植物” or “辨别各种植物.” “吃” is a basic physical action. “尝试” emphasizes testing something to learn about it; “辨别” means identifying differences between plants and highlights Shennong’s knowledge-building purpose.

Word: “很危险的植物” / Context: “他吃了很危险的植物。” / Alternatives: “有毒的植物” or “有害的植物.” “危险” broadly means dangerous, but it does not explain why. “有毒的” specifically means poisonous, which is ideal here; “有害的” means harmful and can describe plants that are bad for health without necessarily being poisonous.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well using a clear chronological structure—first Shennong sees a problem, then he tests plants, writes down information, and talks with friends.  
Next time, focus on using “了” only for completed events and avoiding vague pronouns such as “你” when you mean people in general.

## Scene 2

> 一个人问神农，哪个植物好吃。神农说，这就要看你的口味了。要是你喜欢吃甜的，有很多甜的水果，比方说苹果、西瓜和梨。一个别的人问神农，哪个植物对你的身体有好处。神农说，有很多种植物对你的身体有好处，比方说小白菜、黄瓜、茶和菠菜。一个别的人问神农，哪个植物对你的身体有坏处。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “哪个植物”  
Formula: 哪种 + noun (when asking about a type or category)  
Correct: “哪种植物”  
Use this correction in all three questions: “哪种植物好吃 / 对身体有好处 / 对身体有坏处？”

Incorrect phrase: “这就要看你的口味了”  
Formula: 这就要看 + A 不 A / wh-question / A 还是 B + 了  
Correct: “这就要看你喜欢吃甜的还是酸的了。”  
After “这就要看,” the following part should be a question form, such as “喜欢不喜欢” or “甜的还是酸的.”

Incorrect phrase: “要是你喜欢吃甜的，有很多甜的水果”  
Formula: 要是/如果 + condition，(就) + result  
Correct: “要是你喜欢吃甜的，就可以吃很多甜的水果，比方说苹果、西瓜和梨。”  
The original sentence needs a result after the condition; “就可以吃……” makes the meaning complete.

Incorrect phrase: “一个别的人”  
Formula: 另一个 + noun  
Correct: “另一个人问神农……”  
“一个别的人” is not natural Chinese. Use “另一个人” for “another person.”

Incorrect phrase: “对你的身体有好处 / 对你的身体有坏处”  
Formula: 对 + body/health + 有好处/坏处  
Correct: “哪种植物对身体有好处？” / “哪种植物对身体有坏处？”  
Here, “你的” is unnecessary because the question is about people’s health in general.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “好吃”  
Context: “哪种植物好吃？”  
Alternatives: “可口” or “美味”  
“好吃” is very common and simple. “可口” means tasty and pleasant to eat, while “美味” is stronger and emphasizes that the food tastes especially delicious.

Word: “甜的水果”  
Context: “有很多甜的水果”  
Alternatives: “甜味浓的水果” or “香甜的水果”  
“甜的” simply describes sweetness. “甜味浓的” emphasizes a strong sweet flavor, while “香甜的” suggests both a pleasant fragrance and sweetness.

Word: “比方说”  
Context: “比方说苹果、西瓜和梨”  
Alternatives: “例如” or “比如”  
“比方说” is conversational and natural in speech. “比如” is also common and slightly shorter, while “例如” is more formal and works especially well in writing.

Word: “有好处”  
Context: “对身体有好处”  
Alternatives: “有益” or “有益于健康”  
“有好处” is general and beginner-friendly. “有益” is more concise and formal, and “有益于健康” specifically emphasizes benefits for health.

Word: “有坏处”  
Context: “对身体有坏处”  
Alternatives: “有害” or “不利于健康”  
“有坏处” is a general expression for negative effects. “有害” means harmful and is stronger, while “不利于健康” is a more formal way to say something is bad for health.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well by organizing the passage as a clear question-and-answer conversation and giving specific examples such as apples, watermelon, tea, and spinach.  

Next time, focus on using “哪种 + noun,” “另一个人,” and complete conditional sentences with “要是……，就……”.

## Scene 3

> 神农说，有一天，我吃了一个黄色的花，名字是夹竹桃。我一吃夹竹桃，就觉得这种植物对我的脑有坏处。可是，一个灵芝和一个男人正好在我的旁边，我说，麻烦你把那个植物给我吃一下。我一吃这个灵芝就好了。所以，你得小心吃植物，因为有的植物对你的健康有坏处，特别是夹竹桃。因为夹竹桃有毒。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “我吃了一个黄色的花” / Formula: numeral + measure word + adjective + 的 + noun; for flowers, use 一朵花 / Correct: “我吃了一朵黄色的花。”

Incorrect phrase: “名字是夹竹桃” / Formula: noun + 叫 + name, or noun + 的名字是 + name / Correct: “它叫夹竹桃。”


Incorrect phrase: “我一吃夹竹桃，就觉得这种植物对我的脑有坏处。” / Formula: subject + 一 + verb phrase, 就 + result; use 脑子 rather than 脑, and make the harmful result clear / Correct: “我一吃夹竹桃，就觉得我的脑子不舒服。”  
Note: In this story, the usual effect is that Shennong’s stomach hurts, so “我的肚子就疼了” would be more accurate.


Incorrect phrase: “可是，一个灵芝和一个男人正好在我的旁边” / Formula: person + 正好在 + place; introduce the person clearly, and 灵芝 is not normally described as being “beside” someone / Correct: “可是，正好有一个男人在我旁边，他手里拿着灵芝。”


Incorrect phrase: “麻烦你把那个植物给我吃一下。” / Formula: 麻烦你 + 把 + object + 给我 / 让我 + verb; “给我吃” can sound as if the listener is eating it / Correct: “麻烦你把那个灵芝给我吃一下。”  
More natural: “麻烦你把灵芝给我，让我吃一点儿。”


Incorrect phrase: “我一吃这个灵芝就好了。” / Formula: subject + 一 + verb phrase, 就 + result; use 了 after 吃 to show the completed action / Correct: “我一吃了这个灵芝，身体就好了。”  
More natural: “我吃了灵芝以后，身体就好了。”


Incorrect phrase: “特别是夹竹桃。因为夹竹桃有毒。” / Formula: general statement + 特别是 + specific example; do not begin a separate sentence with 因为 without a result / Correct: “有的植物对健康有坏处，特别是夹竹桃，因为它有毒。”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “有坏处” / Context: “这种植物对我的脑有坏处” / Alternatives: “有害” is more concise and natural for saying that something harms health. “伤害” can be used as a verb, for example, “它会伤害我的身体.”


Word: “脑” / Context: “对我的脑有坏处” / Alternatives: “脑子” is the everyday word for “brain” and sounds more natural in beginner-level narration. However, for this story, “肚子” is more appropriate because oleander makes Shennong’s stomach hurt.


Word: “男人” / Context: “一个灵芝和一个男人正好在我的旁边” / Alternatives: “一个人” is more neutral and natural when the person’s gender is not important. “路人” means a passerby and adds the idea that he happened to be passing by.


Word: “好了” / Context: “我一吃这个灵芝就好了” / Alternatives: “恢复健康了” is more specific and means that someone returned to good health. “好起来了” emphasizes that the condition began to improve.


Word: “小心吃植物” / Context: “你得小心吃植物” / Alternatives: “不要随便吃植物” is more natural and gives a clear warning not to eat plants casually. “谨慎食用不认识的植物” is more formal and emphasizes being careful with unfamiliar plants.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly explained the cause, solution, and lesson of the story, and your use of “一……就……” shows a good understanding of sequence and result.  
Next time, focus on using clear nouns and pronouns so that the reader always knows exactly who or what you mean.
