## Scene 1

> 古代的时候，有一个人叫神农。一天，神农正在路上走，一看很多人正在吃一些植物。一些人一吃植物就生病了，神农想帮别人。所以想知道哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的时候” / Formula: 在 + historical time period / Correct: “在古代”

Incorrect phrase: “神农正在路上走，一看很多人正在吃一些植物” / Formula: Subject + 看到/看见 + object / Correct: “神农正在路上走，看到很多人正在吃一些植物。”

Incorrect phrase: “一些人一吃植物就生病了” / Formula: 有些人 + 一 + verb phrase + 就 + result; use no “了” for a general/habitual situation / Correct: “有些人一吃某些植物就生病。”  
“植物” is too broad here, because not every plant makes people sick; “某些植物” is clearer.

Incorrect phrase: “神农想帮别人。所以想知道……” / Formula: Subject + 想 + verb, 所以 + verb phrase / Correct: “神农想帮助人们，所以想知道哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃。”  
“所以想知道” needs to be connected to the previous clause rather than standing as a separate sentence.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “路上走” / Context: “神农正在路上走” / Alternatives: “赶路” means travelling somewhere with a purpose and sounds more natural in a story. “行走” is more formal and literary, emphasizing the act of walking.

Word: “一些人” / Context: “一些人一吃植物就生病” / Alternatives: “有些人” is more natural when referring to an unspecified group of people. It means “some people,” often in contrast with other people.

Word: “生病” / Context: “一吃植物就生病” / Alternatives: “病倒” suggests that they became seriously ill, perhaps too weak to continue walking. “身体不舒服” is milder and means they felt unwell.

Word: “帮别人” / Context: “神农想帮别人” / Alternatives: “帮助人们” sounds more natural and more fitting for a story about Shennong helping the public. “救治病人” is more specific: it means treating ill people.

Word: “不能吃” / Context: “哪些植物不能吃” / Alternatives: “有毒” means poisonous and clearly explains why a plant should not be eaten. “不可食用” means inedible/not suitable for eating and is more formal.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did very well introducing 神农 clearly at the beginning and creating a logical story sequence: people get sick, and then Shennong wants to find a solution.  
Next time, focus on linking related ideas with connectors such as “所以” and choosing specific nouns such as “某些植物” instead of general ones.

## Scene 2

> 然后，神农开始尝很多植物，比方说他吃过生姜、芥兰和菠菜。一些植物吃真好，特别是黄瓜。但是，一些植物毒得很，特别是夹竹桃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “神农开始尝很多植物” / Formula: Subject + 开始 + 尝试/品尝 + (各种/很多) + noun / Correct: “神农开始尝试各种植物。”  
“尝” can mean “to taste,” but “尝试各种植物” is more natural when he is testing whether plants are edible.

Incorrect phrase: “一些植物吃真好” / Formula: Subject + 吃起来 + adjective / Correct: “一些植物吃起来真好。”  
Use **吃起来** to describe how food tastes when eaten.

Incorrect phrase: “一些植物毒得很” / Formula: Subject + 很/非常 + adjective; or Subject + 有毒 / Correct: “一些植物很有毒。”  
“毒得很” is understandable, but **很有毒** is the more natural way to describe poisonous plants.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “比方说” / Context: “比方说他吃过生姜、芥兰和菠菜” / Alternatives: **例如** or **比如**. “比方说” is conversational and often introduces a full example. “例如” is slightly more formal and works well in writing; “比如” is natural and common in both speech and writing.

Word: “尝” / Context: “神农开始尝很多植物” / Alternatives: **尝试** or **品尝**. “尝” is short and can mean tasting a small amount. “尝试” emphasizes experimenting or testing plants, which fits Shennong’s purpose. “品尝” emphasizes carefully tasting food for its flavor.

Word: “真好” / Context: “一些植物吃起来真好” / Alternatives: **很好吃**, **味道不错**, or **美味**. “真好” is understandable but vague. “很好吃” is the most direct everyday description; “味道不错” is a softer, more natural evaluation; “美味” is more descriptive and literary.

Word: “毒得很” / Context: “一些植物毒得很” / Alternatives: **很有毒**, **有毒性**, or **有害**. “毒得很” stresses a high degree of poison but is less idiomatic here. “很有毒” is the most natural everyday phrase; “有毒性” is more scientific; “有害” can refer more broadly to something harmful, not only poisonous.

Word: “特别是” / Context: “特别是黄瓜 / 特别是夹竹桃” / Alternatives: **尤其是** or **其中**. “特别是” is clear and common. “尤其是” gives stronger emphasis and is slightly more polished. “其中，夹竹桃是有毒的” is useful when you want to identify one item from a group more specifically.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You used a clear narrative sequence and gave effective contrasting examples of healthy and poisonous plants.  
Next time, focus on using result or sensory complements such as **吃起来很好吃** and more natural descriptions such as **很有毒**.

## Scene 3

> 一个人对神农说，麻烦我们可以吃什么植物？神农说，这就要看你想不想生病了。以后，神农告诉别人一些植物能吃，一些植物不能吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “麻烦我们可以吃什么植物？” / Formula: 请问/麻烦问一下 + question / Correct: “请问，我们可以吃什么植物？”  
“麻烦” by itself does not introduce a question naturally; use 请问 or 麻烦问一下.

Incorrect phrase: “这就要看你想不想生病了。” / Formula: (能不能吃 / 会不会生病) + 这就要看 + A-not-A question + 了 / Correct: “什么植物能吃，这就要看吃了会不会生病了。”  
“这就要看……” should answer an uncertain condition, and the part after 看 needs a question form. “想不想生病” means “whether you want to get sick,” which is not the relevant condition here.

Incorrect phrase: “以后，神农告诉别人一些植物能吃，一些植物不能吃。” / Formula: 后来 + subject + 告诉 + specific person/group + content / Correct: “后来，神农告诉人们：一些植物能吃，一些植物不能吃。”  
“别人” is vague here; “人们” clearly refers to the general public. A colon also makes the reported information clearer.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “一个人” / Context: “一个人对神农说” / Alternatives: “有个人” or “一位村民”  
“一个人” is grammatically possible but very general. “有个人” is more natural when introducing someone in a story, while “一位村民” makes the character more specific and gives the story a clearer setting.

Word: “麻烦” / Context: “麻烦我们可以吃什么植物？” / Alternatives: “请问” or “麻烦问一下”  
“麻烦” usually means “trouble” or is used in requests such as “麻烦你帮我…”. “请问” is the standard polite expression for asking a question; “麻烦问一下” is polite and conversational.

Word: “告诉” / Context: “神农告诉别人” / Alternatives: “告诉” or “教给”  
“告诉” means to inform someone of facts. “教给” emphasizes teaching knowledge or skills, so “神农教给人们怎么分辨能吃和不能吃的植物” would show that Shennong is teaching people.

Word: “能吃” / Context: “一些植物能吃” / Alternatives: “可以食用” or “无毒”  
“能吃” is simple and appropriate for a beginner. “可以食用” is more formal and precise, meaning “edible.” “无毒” specifically means “non-poisonous,” so it focuses on safety rather than whether something is normally eaten.

Word: “不能吃” / Context: “一些植物不能吃” / Alternatives: “有毒” or “不宜食用”  
“不能吃” is broad: it may mean poisonous, unpleasant, or unsuitable. “有毒” clearly states that a plant is poisonous, while “不宜食用” is a more formal expression meaning “not suitable for eating.”

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly expressed the important contrast between edible and inedible plants, and your short dialogue has a logical story flow.  
Next time, focus on using “这就要看……” with an uncertain question such as “会不会生病” or “能不能吃.”
