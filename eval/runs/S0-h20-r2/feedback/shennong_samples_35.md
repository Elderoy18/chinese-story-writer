## Scene 1

> 在古代，很多医生不知道怎么用药。所以他们找到了很多植物对药。神农先看到很多病人，他们吃很多植物对药。但是我觉得他们的吃的植物，让他们病人。神农觉得这个植物可能特别是毒。所以他想找一个植物对药，他们可以吃对药。正好，他找到了一把花，他想把花放在茶里，对药。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “他们找到了很多植物对药” / Formula: Subject + 找到 + [可以做药 / 可以入药的] + noun / Correct: “他们找到了很多可以入药的植物。”

Incorrect phrase: “神农先看到很多病人，他们吃很多植物对药” / Formula: Subject + 看到 + object; then use a clear subject + 吃了 + object / Correct: “神农先看到了很多病人。他们吃了很多药用植物。”  
“他们” is unclear here: it could mean the doctors or the patients. Use “病人” again if needed.

Incorrect phrase: “但是我觉得他们的吃的植物，让他们病人” / Formula: Subject + 吃的 + noun + 让 + person + 生病 / Correct: “但是神农觉得他们吃的植物让他们生病了。”  
In a story, “神农觉得” is clearer than “我觉得.” Also, say “生病,” not “病人” (which means “patient”).

Incorrect phrase: “神农觉得这个植物可能特别是毒” / Formula: Subject + 觉得 + noun + 可能 + 有毒 / Correct: “神农觉得这些植物可能有毒。”  
Use “有毒” to describe something poisonous. “特别是” means “especially,” so it does not fit before “毒.”

Incorrect phrase: “所以他想找一个植物对药，他们可以吃对药” / Formula: Subject + 想找 + 一些 + 可以做药的 + noun; people + 可以 + 吃 / Correct: “所以他想找一些可以做药的植物，让病人吃。”  
“一个植物” is unnatural when speaking generally; use “一些植物.” Also, “对药” should be “做药” or “入药.”

Incorrect phrase: “正好，他找到了一把花，他想把花放在茶里，对药” / Formula: 然后/正好 + subject + 找到了 + object; 把 + object + 泡在 + drink + 里; 用来 + 做药 / Correct: “正好，他找到了一朵花，想把花泡在茶里，用来做药。”  
“一把花” usually means a bunch of flowers. For one flower, say “一朵花.”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “医生” / Context: “很多医生不知道怎么用药” / Alternatives: “大夫” is a more traditional and natural word for a doctor in an ancient-story setting. “医者” is more literary and formal, often used when discussing healers in historical contexts. “医生” is correct but sounds more modern.

Word: “用药” / Context: “不知道怎么用药” / Alternatives: “配药” means preparing and combining medicinal ingredients, while “治病” means treating illness. “用药” focuses generally on using medicine; “配药” is more specific if the doctors are making medicine from plants.

Word: “很多植物” / Context: “找到了很多植物” / Alternatives: “药草” means medicinal herbs and is a precise word for plants used as medicine. “草药” refers to herbal medicine, often including the herbs after they are collected or prepared. These are more natural than repeatedly saying “植物.”

Word: “有毒” / Context: “这些植物可能有毒” / Alternatives: “毒性很强” means the plant’s toxicity is strong and sounds more precise. “有害” means harmful, but it is broader and does not necessarily mean poisonous. “有毒” is the clearest basic choice.

Word: “放在茶里” / Context: “把花放在茶里” / Alternatives: “泡在茶里” means steeping something in tea or hot water, which is the natural action for making a drink. “煮成药汤” means boiling ingredients into a medicinal soup/decoction and better fits traditional Chinese medicine.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well creating a clear cause-and-effect story: the people eat plants, become ill, and Shennong tries to find safer medicinal plants.  
Next time, focus on using clear subjects (“病人,” “医生,” or “神农”) and the useful phrases “做药 / 入药 / 有毒.”

## Scene 2

> 他不知道如果这把花对你的身体好，这就要看如果这把花毒不毒了。神农想找别的植物，但是这很麻烦。正好，他找到了别的花。他一吃花，很快就生病了。他的肚子疼死了。神农不知道他吃不吃灵芝或者夹竹桃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “他不知道如果这把花对你的身体好”  
Formula: 谁 + 不知道 + embedded yes/no question (A-not-A), not “如果”  
Correct: “神农不知道这束花对身体有没有好处。”  
Use “有没有好处” for “whether it is good,” and specify 神农 rather than using an unclear “他.” Also, “你的身体” changes the perspective unexpectedly.

Incorrect phrase: “这把花”  
Formula: 一 + measure word + noun  
Correct: “这束花”  
“把” is not normally used for flowers; use “束” for a bouquet/bunch of flowers.

Incorrect phrase: “这就要看如果这把花毒不毒了”  
Formula: (uncertain result)，这就要看 + A-not-A question + 了  
Correct: “这就要看这束花有没有毒了。”  
After “这就要看,” use a question form such as “有没有毒,” not “如果.”

Incorrect phrase: “但是这很麻烦”  
Formula: specific noun/action + 很 + adjective  
Correct: “但是找新的植物很麻烦。”  
“这” is too vague here. Say exactly what is difficult: finding new plants.

Incorrect phrase: “正好，他找到了别的花”  
Formula: 恰好/正好 + subject + found + 一种/另一种 + noun  
Correct: “恰好，他找到了一种花。”  
“别的花” needs a clearer reference—different from which flower? “一种花” introduces the flower naturally.

Incorrect phrase: “他一吃花，很快就生病了”  
Formula: 谁 + 一 + verb + 这种/那种 + noun，+ 就 + result  
Correct: “他一吃这种花，很快就生病了。”  
Use “这种花” to make clear that it is the flower he just found.

Incorrect phrase: “他的肚子疼死了”  
Formula: subject + 疼得 + degree/complement  
Correct: “他的肚子疼得很厉害。”  
“疼死了” can literally suggest that he died, which does not fit the following sentence.

Incorrect phrase: “神农不知道他吃不吃灵芝或者夹竹桃”  
Formula: 谁 + 不知道 + subject + 吃的是 + A 还是 B  
Correct: “神农不知道自己吃的是灵芝还是夹竹桃。”  
“他” is ambiguous because it could refer to someone other than Shennong. For two alternatives, use “还是,” not “或者.”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “这把花”  
Context: “这把花对你的身体好”  
Alternatives: “这束花” means a bunch or bouquet of flowers. If Shennong is testing one type of plant, “这种花” is even better because it means “this kind of flower.”

Word: “麻烦”  
Context: “但是这很麻烦”  
Alternatives: “困难” means “difficult” and sounds more neutral and formal. “不容易” is a natural, simple way to say that finding new plants is not easy. “麻烦” can also mean something annoying or troublesome.

Word: “正好”  
Context: “正好，他找到了别的花”  
Alternatives: “恰好” means “just by chance” and is slightly more literary. “碰巧” emphasizes coincidence: he happened to find a flower.

Word: “别的花”  
Context: “他找到了别的花”  
Alternatives: “一种花” is best when introducing a new flower for the first time. “另一种花” means “another kind of flower,” but it should only be used when the first kind has already been clearly mentioned.

Word: “肚子疼死了”  
Context: “他的肚子疼死了”  
Alternatives: “肚子疼得厉害” means his stomach hurt badly, without suggesting death. “腹痛” is a more formal word for stomach pain and is useful in medical or written contexts.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well creating a clear cause-and-effect story: Shennong finds a flower, eats it, and then becomes ill.  
Next time, focus on using “有没有……,” “还是,” and clear nouns such as “这种花” instead of vague pronouns like “这” or “他.”

## Scene 3

> 别的人给了神农一个灵芝。这个植物可以让神农更好。比方说，他会觉得更健康。吃灵芝以后，他可以再走。现在他觉得更好。最后，神农找到了很多植物，他可以用对药。这些植物让神农很高兴。神农想跟很多人分享他的新药。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “别的人给了神农一个灵芝。” / Formula: 人 + 给 + 人 + （数量）+ 量词 + 名词; use a specific subject rather than vague “别的人” / Correct: “别人给了神农一株灵芝。”  
“灵芝” is usually counted with 株 rather than 个.

Incorrect phrase: “这个植物可以让神农更好。” / Formula: 药物/植物 + 让 + 人 + 身体/感觉 + 更好; or 对 + 人 + 有好处 / Correct: “这种植物可以让神农感觉更好。”  
“让神农更好” is incomplete because “更好” needs to describe something specific, such as his health or how he feels.

Incorrect phrase: “比方说，他会觉得更健康。” / Formula: For a specific result after an action, 主语 + 会/会觉得 + 更 + 形容词; use “比如说” to introduce an example naturally / Correct: “比如说，他会觉得更健康。”  
This is understandable, but “比如说” is more natural in this context.

Incorrect phrase: “吃灵芝以后，他可以再走。” / Formula: 吃了 + 名词 + 以后，主语 + 又/可以继续 + 动词 / Correct: “吃了灵芝以后，神农又可以继续走了。”  
Use 了 after 吃 for the completed action. “继续走” makes the meaning clearer: he is able to keep walking.

Incorrect phrase: “现在他觉得更好。” / Formula: 主语 + 觉得 + 身体/状态 + 更好; avoid an unclear pronoun when the name can be repeated / Correct: “现在，神农觉得身体好多了。”  
“更好” needs a clearer reference, and “好多了” is natural for recovery.

Incorrect phrase: “最后，神农找到了很多植物，他可以用对药。” / Formula: 人 + 找到 + 很多 + 能/可以 + 做药的 + 植物; or 人 + 用 + 植物 + 做药 / Correct: “最后，神农找到了很多可以做药的植物。”  
“用对药” is not a natural verb-object combination. You can say “用植物做药.”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “别的人” / Context: “别的人给了神农一个灵芝。” / Alternatives: “别人” is the natural everyday term for “other people.” “一位老人” or “一位村民” would make the story more vivid and specific if you know who gave him the lingzhi.

Word: “这个植物” / Context: “这个植物可以让神农更好。” / Alternatives: “这种植物” is more natural when referring to a type of plant. “灵芝” is even clearer because it directly repeats the important item in the story.

Word: “更好” / Context: “神农觉得更好。” / Alternatives: “好多了” naturally means “much better,” especially after being ill or tired. “恢复了精神” means “regained his energy” and gives a more vivid description of his condition.

Word: “走” / Context: “他可以再走。” / Alternatives: “继续赶路” means “continue his journey” and fits a travel story well. “重新上路” means “set off again” after a stop or difficulty.

Word: “新药” / Context: “神农想跟很多人分享他的新药。” / Alternatives: “药材” refers to medicinal materials, such as plants used to make medicine. “草药” specifically refers to herbal medicine and fits lingzhi and other healing plants very well.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well using a clear time sequence—eating lingzhi, feeling better, finding plants, and sharing medicine—so your story is easy to follow.  
Next time, focus on making pronouns and descriptions more specific, especially by saying what becomes “better” and how plants are used as medicine.
