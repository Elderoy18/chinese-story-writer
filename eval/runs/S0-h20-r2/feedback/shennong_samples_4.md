## Scene 1

> 古代的时候，有一个男人，他的名字是神农。他看到了有很多人不知道哪个植物好吃，也不知道什么植物对你的身体有好处。这个问题神农觉得很糟糕，所以神农去了吃每种植物。他吃了一种植物以后，就写了这个植物对你有没有好处。有的时候，神农吃了很好吃的植物。有的时候，他吃了很危险的植物。他吃完了每种植物以后，会来跟他的朋友聊到他吃的植物了。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的时候” / Formula: 在 + historical time period / Correct: “在古代”

Incorrect phrase: “哪个植物好吃，也不知道什么植物对你的身体有好处” / Formula: 哪些 + plural noun；对 + [person/people’s] + 身体 + 有好处 / Correct: “哪些植物可以吃，也不知道什么植物对人的身体有好处。”  
“哪个” asks about one choice, but people do not know *which plants* are edible, so “哪些” is better. Also, “你” suddenly addresses the reader; use “人” or “他们” instead.

Incorrect phrase: “所以神农去了吃每种植物” / Formula: Subject + 尝/吃 + 每一种/各种 + noun / Correct: “所以神农尝了每一种植物。”  
“去” needs a destination or a purpose structure, such as “去山里找植物.” Here, 神农 directly tries the plants.

Incorrect phrase: “就写了这个植物对你有没有好处” / Formula: Subject + 写下 + 这种/这些 + noun + 对 + 人的身体 + 有没有好处 / Correct: “就写下这种植物对人的身体有没有好处。”  
Use “写下” for recording information. “这种植物” refers naturally to the plant he has just eaten.

Incorrect phrase: “有的时候，神农吃了很好吃的植物” / Formula: 有的时候 + subject + verb (for habitual actions; no 了) / Correct: “有的时候，神农会吃到很好吃的植物。”  
This describes a repeated situation, so do not use “了.” “会吃到” expresses that he sometimes happens to find delicious plants.

Incorrect phrase: “他吃完了每种植物以后，会来跟他的朋友聊到他吃的植物了” / Formula: 吃完 + noun + 以后，subject + 会 + 跟 + person + 聊/讨论 + topic / Correct: “他吃完每一种植物以后，会跟朋友聊聊他尝过的植物。”  
For a habitual action, “会” should not be combined with final “了.” “来” is unnecessary because there is no important direction of movement.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “看到了” / Context: “他看到了有很多人不知道……” / Alternatives: “发现” or “注意到.” “看到” means physically seeing something, while “发现” means realizing an important fact or problem. “注意到” means noticing something that may have been overlooked. Here, “发现” is the most natural choice.

Word: “好吃” / Context: “哪个植物好吃” / Alternatives: “可以吃的” or “可食用的.” “好吃” means delicious, but the main issue is whether a plant is safe to eat. “可以吃的” is clear and common; “可食用的” is more formal and is often used in science-related contexts.

Word: “糟糕” / Context: “这个问题神农觉得很糟糕” / Alternatives: “严重” or “棘手.” “糟糕” is a general word for something bad. “严重” emphasizes that the problem has important consequences, while “棘手” emphasizes that it is difficult to solve.

Word: “吃每种植物” / Context: “神农……吃每种植物” / Alternatives: “尝试各种植物” or “品尝各种植物.” “吃” is simple and direct. “尝试” emphasizes testing plants to learn about them; “品尝” emphasizes tasting them carefully. For Shennong’s experiment, “尝试” works especially well.

Word: “危险的植物” / Context: “他吃了很危险的植物” / Alternatives: “有毒的植物” or “有害的植物.” “危险” is broad: it could describe any dangerous situation. “有毒” specifically means poisonous, while “有害” means harmful to health without necessarily being poisonous.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well in presenting the story in a clear chronological order, and your contrast between beneficial and dangerous plants makes Shennong’s purpose easy to understand.  
Next time, focus on distinguishing repeated habits from completed actions, especially when to omit or use “了.”

## Scene 2

> 一个人问神农，哪个植物好吃。神农说，这就要看你的口味了。要是你喜欢吃甜的，有很多甜的水果，比方说苹果、西瓜和梨。一个别的人问神农，哪个植物对你的身体有好处。神农说，有很多种植物对你的身体有好处，比方说小白菜、黄瓜、茶和菠菜。一个别的人问神农，哪个植物对你的身体有坏处。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “哪个植物好吃” / Formula: 哪种 + noun + adjective / Correct: “哪种植物好吃？”

Incorrect phrase: “这就要看你的口味了” / Formula: 这就要看 + embedded question (A-not-A / 是……还是……) + 了 / Correct: “这就要看你喜欢吃甜的还是不甜的了。”  
“这就要看……” needs a question form after 看, rather than just a noun phrase.

Incorrect phrase: “要是你喜欢吃甜的，有很多甜的水果” / Formula: 要是/如果 + condition，(就) + result / Correct: “要是你喜欢吃甜的，就可以吃很多甜的水果。”  
Adding 就 makes the condition-and-result relationship clear.

Incorrect phrase: “一个别的人” / Formula: 一个……，另一个…… / Correct: “另一个人问神农，哪种植物对身体有好处？”

Incorrect phrase: “一个别的人问神农，哪个植物对你的身体有坏处” / Formula: 另一个人 + 问 + person，哪种 + noun + 对身体 + 有害 / Correct: “另一个人问神农，哪种植物对身体有害？”  
“另一个人” is the natural way to introduce another person, and “哪种植物” is more natural than “哪个植物.”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “好吃” / Context: “哪种植物好吃？” / Alternatives: “可口” or “美味.” 好吃 is very common and conversational. 可口 means tasty and pleasant to eat, while 美味 is stronger and emphasizes that the food is especially delicious.

Word: “甜的” / Context: “喜欢吃甜的” / Alternatives: “甜食” or “甜味的食物.” 甜的 is understandable but informal because the noun is omitted. 甜食 specifically means sweet foods, while 甜味的食物 is clearer and more descriptive.

Word: “有好处” / Context: “对你的身体有好处” / Alternatives: “有益” or “有营养.” 有好处 is a broad everyday expression. 有益 means beneficial, especially for health, while 有营养 emphasizes nutritional value.

Word: “有坏处” / Context: “对你的身体有坏处” / Alternatives: “有害” or “不利于健康.” 有坏处 is understandable but general. 有害 means harmful and is concise; 不利于健康 means “not good for one’s health” and sounds more formal.

Word: “比方说” / Context: “比方说苹果、西瓜和梨” / Alternatives: “例如” or “比如.” 比方说 is natural in speech. 比如 is also conversational and shorter, while 例如 is more formal and works well in writing.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did well by organizing the scene as a clear question-and-answer conversation and giving relevant examples of fruits and vegetables.  

Next time, focus on using “哪种” with categories such as plants and on completing every question with an answer.

## Scene 3

> 神农说，有一天，我吃了一个黄色的花，名字是夹竹桃。我一吃夹竹桃，就觉得这种植物对我的脑有坏处。可是，一个灵芝和一个男人正好在我的旁边，我说，麻烦你把那个植物给我吃一下。我一吃这个灵芝就好了。所以，你得小心吃植物，因为有的植物对你的健康有坏处，特别是夹竹桃。因为夹竹桃有毒。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “一个黄色的花” / Formula: 一 + classifier + adjective + noun / Correct: “一朵黄色的花”  
Use 朵 for flowers.

Incorrect phrase: “名字是夹竹桃” / Formula: Noun + 叫 + name / Correct: “它叫夹竹桃” or “这种花叫夹竹桃”  
“叫” is more natural when introducing the name of a flower or plant.

Incorrect phrase: “这种植物对我的脑有坏处” / Formula: 对 + body part + 有害 / Correct: “这种植物对我的大脑有害”  
However, according to the story, oleander causes Shennong’s stomach to hurt, so an even better correction is: “我一吃夹竹桃，肚子就疼了。”

Incorrect phrase: “一个灵芝和一个男人正好在我的旁边” / Formula: Person + 有 + medicine/object / Correct: “正好有一个男人在我旁边，他有灵芝。”  
A 灵芝 cannot naturally stand “next to” someone in the same way a person does. Also, the man should be introduced clearly.

Incorrect phrase: “麻烦你把那个植物给我吃一下” / Formula: 麻烦 + person + 把 + specific object + 给 + person / Correct: “麻烦你把灵芝给我吃一下。”  
“那个植物” is vague here; use “灵芝” to make the reference clear.

Incorrect phrase: “我一吃这个灵芝就好了” / Formula: Subject + 一 + verb + object + 就 + result / Correct: “我一吃灵芝，肚子就不疼了。”  
This gives a clearer result than simply “好了.”

Incorrect phrase: “特别是夹竹桃。因为夹竹桃有毒。” / Formula: General statement + 特别是 + example + 因为 + reason / Correct: “有的植物对健康有害，特别是夹竹桃，因为它有毒。”  
“特别是夹竹桃” correctly gives an example, but “因为夹竹桃有毒” should be connected to the same sentence. Use “它” to avoid repeating 夹竹桃.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “有坏处” / Context: “对我的脑有坏处” / Alternatives: “有害” or “伤害”  
“有坏处” is understandable but quite general. “对大脑有害” is more concise and formal. “伤害大脑” emphasizes the action or effect of causing damage.

Word: “脑” / Context: “对我的脑有坏处” / Alternatives: “大脑” or “身体”  
“脑” is informal and usually sounds incomplete in this context. “大脑” is the standard word for the brain. If you mean overall health, “身体” is more natural: “对身体有害。”

Word: “好了” / Context: “我一吃这个灵芝就好了” / Alternatives: “恢复了” or “不疼了”  
“好了” is common but vague: it does not say what improved. “不疼了” clearly describes the symptom disappearing, while “恢复了” means that someone returned to a healthy condition and sounds slightly more formal.

Word: “小心吃植物” / Context: “你得小心吃植物” / Alternatives: “不要随便吃植物” or “吃植物要谨慎”  
“小心” means to be careful, but “不要随便吃” is more natural advice when warning someone not to eat unknown plants casually. “谨慎” is more formal and emphasizes careful judgment.

Word: “有毒” / Context: “夹竹桃有毒” / Alternatives: “有毒性” or “是有毒植物”  
“有毒” is direct and very natural in everyday speech. “有毒性” is more scientific and formal, while “有毒植物” is useful when classifying oleander as a dangerous kind of plant.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You did a good job using a clear cause-and-effect story: Shennong eats oleander, feels sick, and then uses lingzhi to recover.  
Next time, focus on using specific nouns instead of vague phrases like “那个植物,” and connect short sentences more smoothly.
