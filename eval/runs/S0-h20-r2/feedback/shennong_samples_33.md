## Scene 1

> 古代时候，人不知道他们能吃什么植物，常常一吃就死。但是不知道为什么，没有办法。所以有一天，神农出去试一试很多种植物，告诉人什么植物是安全什么是危险。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代时候，人不知道他们能吃什么植物” / Formula: 在 + time, + people + 不知道 + embedded question / Correct: “在古代，人们不知道什么植物能吃。”  
“人” is too vague here, and “他们” has an unclear referent. “人们” is more natural.

Incorrect phrase: “常常一吃就死” / Formula: subject + 一 + verb phrase + 就 + result / Correct: “人们常常一吃错植物就会死。”  
Add a clear subject and object. “吃错植物” explains what causes the danger.

Incorrect phrase: “但是不知道为什么，没有办法” / Formula: subject + 不知道 + question word + verb; therefore + result / Correct: “但是人们不知道为什么，也没有办法解决这个问题。”  
The subject is missing, and “没有办法” is incomplete without saying what they cannot solve.

Incorrect phrase: “神农出去试一试很多种植物” / Formula: subject + 去/出去 + verb + object / Correct: “神农出去尝试很多种植物。”  
“试一试” is usually followed by a specific action or thing and sounds less natural here. For plants, “尝试” or “品尝” works better.

Incorrect phrase: “告诉人什么植物是安全什么是危险” / Formula: 告诉 + person + embedded statement/question / Correct: “告诉人们什么植物是安全的，什么植物是危险的。”  
Use “人们” rather than vague “人,” and add 的 after adjectives used to describe plants.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “古代时候” / Context: “古代时候，人不知道……” / Alternatives: “在古代” is the most natural and concise way to set a story in ancient times. “很久以前” means “a long time ago” and has a more storytelling-like tone.

Word: “死” / Context: “常常一吃就死” / Alternatives: “丧命” is more formal and literary, meaning to lose one’s life. “中毒而死” is especially precise here because the cause is poisonous plants. “死” is direct and common, but can sound abrupt in a narrative.

Word: “不知道为什么” / Context: “但是不知道为什么” / Alternatives: “不明白其中的原因” sounds more formal and emphasizes that people do not understand the cause. “不清楚原因” is natural and neutral.

Word: “试一试” / Context: “神农出去试一试很多种植物” / Alternatives: “尝试” means to try or test something and is natural for an experiment. “品尝” specifically means to taste food or plants. “试一试” is casual and is better for trying a particular activity or object.

Word: “安全、危险” / Context: “什么植物是安全什么是危险” / Alternatives: “无毒” means non-poisonous and is more precise for plants. “有毒” means poisonous. These words are clearer than “安全/危险” when Shennong is identifying edible plants.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You created a clear cause-and-effect story: people could not identify safe plants, so Shennong tested them to help everyone.  
Next time, focus on making the subject clear in every sentence and using more specific words for the plants’ effects.

## Scene 2

> 发现有的植物对身体非常好，特别是茶，让肚子感觉又暖和又清楚。他告诉朋友：“你吃哪种植物，这就要看你的口味。比方说，你爱吃甜的，可能喜欢糖。”他最喜欢吃花，除了夹竹桃以外，什么都花想吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “发现有的植物对身体非常好”  
Formula: specific person/name + 发现 + clause. Use a clear subject before introducing 他.  
Correct: “神农发现有的植物对身体非常好。” Then you can write “他告诉朋友……”


Incorrect phrase: “你吃哪种植物，这就要看你的口味。”  
Formula: uncertain question + 这就要看 + A-not-A / wh-question / A还是B + 了. The part after 看 needs a question form, not just a noun phrase.  
Correct: “你应该吃哪种植物，这就要看你喜欢吃甜的还是咸的了。”


Incorrect phrase: “什么都花想吃”  
Formula: 什么 + noun + 都 + 想/要 + verb.  
Correct: “除了夹竹桃以外，什么花都想吃。”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “非常好”  
Context: “有的植物对身体非常好”  
Alternatives: “对身体很有益” or “有益健康.” “非常好” is general and conversational; “有益” and “有益健康” are more precise for describing health benefits.


Word: “茶”  
Context: “特别是茶”  
Alternatives: “茶叶” or “茶水.” “茶” can refer to either the plant, leaves, or drink. “茶叶” is clearer if you mean the plant material, while “茶水” refers to the prepared drink.


Word: “暖和”  
Context: “让肚子感觉又暖和又清楚”  
Alternatives: “暖胃” or “舒服.” “暖和” usually describes weather, rooms, or clothing; “暖胃” specifically means warming and comforting the stomach.


Word: “清楚”  
Context: “让肚子感觉……清楚”  
Alternatives: “舒服” or “让人头脑清醒.” “清楚” describes something understandable or a clear mind, not a stomach. Use “舒服” for the stomach, or “头脑清醒” if you mean tea helps someone think clearly.


Word: “糖”  
Context: “你爱吃甜的，可能喜欢糖”  
Alternatives: “甜食” or “糖果.” “糖” usually means sugar as an ingredient, while “甜食” means sweet foods in general and “糖果” means candy.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You used a clear progression—from discovering helpful plants, to giving advice, to sharing a personal preference—and your example with tea makes the scene easy to follow.  
Next time, focus on introducing the main character clearly and using “这就要看……” with a question form after 看.

## Scene 3

> 他发现这个口味，一百岁时候，不小心吃了毒的花。一吃就开始死。但是生病时候告诉人什么植物是药，什么植物是毒。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “一百岁时候” / Formula: 在 + 年龄 + 的时候 / Correct: “在一百岁的时候”

Incorrect phrase: “毒的花” / Formula: 有毒的 + 名词 / Correct: “有毒的花”  
More naturally in this story: “有毒的植物”

Incorrect phrase: “一吃就开始死” / Formula: 主语 + 一 + 动词 + 就 + 结果 / Correct: “他一吃就中毒了” or “他一吃，身体就开始不舒服了”  
“开始死” is not natural Chinese; use “中毒” or describe the symptoms instead.

Incorrect phrase: “但是生病时候告诉人” / Formula: 在 + 情况/时间 + 的时候，主语 + 告诉 + 人们 + 信息 / Correct: “但是，他生病的时候，仍然告诉人们什么植物是药，什么植物是毒。”  
Adding “仍然” makes the contrast with “但是” clear.

A smoother full version: “他发现了这些植物的味道。在一百岁的时候，他不小心吃了有毒的植物，一吃就中毒了。但是，他生病的时候，仍然告诉人们什么植物是药，什么植物是毒。”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “口味” / Context: “他发现这个口味” / Alternatives: “味道” or “药性”  
“口味” usually means someone’s food preference, such as “我喜欢辣的口味.” “味道” means the actual taste or smell of something. If you mean whether a plant can heal or poison people, “药性” (medicinal properties) is more precise.

Word: “花” / Context: “吃了毒的花” / Alternatives: “植物” or “草药”  
“花” specifically means a flower. “植物” is broader and fits a story about tasting many kinds of plants. “草药” emphasizes plants used as medicine.

Word: “死” / Context: “开始死” / Alternatives: “中毒” or “生命垂危”  
“死” is very direct and describes death itself. “中毒” means “to be poisoned” and is the best word for what happens immediately after eating a poisonous plant. “生命垂危” means “to be in critical condition” and is more dramatic and literary.

Word: “告诉人” / Context: “告诉人什么植物是药” / Alternatives: “告诉人们” or “教人们分辨”  
“告诉人” is understandable, but “告诉人们” sounds more complete when speaking generally. “教人们分辨什么植物有毒、什么植物可以入药” means teaching people how to distinguish plants, which is more vivid and specific.

Word: “药” / Context: “什么植物是药” / Alternatives: “药材” or “可以治病的植物”  
“药” is a general word for medicine. “药材” refers specifically to medicinal materials, often plants used to make medicine. “可以治病的植物” is simple and very clear for a beginner-level story.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You communicated the important contrast clearly: even when he was poisoned, he helped others learn which plants were safe.  
Next time, focus on adding “在……的时候” for time expressions and using more precise words such as “中毒” and “植物.”
