## Scene 1

> 古代的时候，一个男人觉得植物很有意思。他叫神农。有的植物吃了以后他的肚子疼死了，可是有的吃了以后很舒服。他找很多植物。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的时候，一个男人觉得植物很有意思。他叫神农。”  
Formula: 在 + historical time，+ character’s name + identity/introduction + action  
Correct: “在古代，有一个叫神农的人，他对植物很感兴趣。”  
“在古代” is more natural than “古代的时候,” and it is clearer to introduce 神农 by name when he first appears.

Incorrect phrase: “有的植物吃了以后他的肚子疼死了”  
Formula: 有的 + noun，+ subject + 吃了以后，+ result / subject + 疼得 + degree  
Correct: “有的植物他吃了以后，肚子疼得要命。”  
The original word order makes the subject slightly unclear. Also, “肚子疼死了” can literally suggest that he died; “疼得要命” means “extremely painful.”

Incorrect phrase: “可是有的吃了以后很舒服。”  
Formula: 有的 + noun，+ subject + 吃了以后，+ feeling/result  
Correct: “可是有的植物他吃了以后，觉得很舒服。”  
After “有的,” repeat “植物” for clarity, and add “觉得” because “舒服” describes a person’s feeling.

Incorrect phrase: “他找很多植物。”  
Formula: subject + verb + 了 + quantity + noun  
Correct: “他找了很多植物。”  
Use “了” here because this is a completed action in the story.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “很有意思”  
Context: “植物很有意思”  
Alternatives: “很有趣” or “很神奇.” “有意思” is common and conversational. “有趣” is a natural neutral choice for something interesting, while “神奇” emphasizes that plants seem amazing or mysterious.

Word: “肚子疼死了”  
Context: “他的肚子疼死了”  
Alternatives: “肚子疼得厉害” or “肚子疼得要命.” “疼死了” is informal and can sound like he literally died. “疼得厉害” is clear and neutral; “疼得要命” is stronger and more expressive.

Word: “舒服”  
Context: “吃了以后很舒服”  
Alternatives: “感觉很好” or “身体舒服多了.” “舒服” usually describes physical comfort. “感觉很好” is broader, while “身体舒服多了” emphasizes that his physical condition improved.

Word: “找”  
Context: “他找了很多植物”  
Alternatives: “寻找” or “采集.” “找” simply means “look for.” “寻找” is slightly more formal and story-like; “采集” means to collect plants, which is especially suitable for Shennong’s activity.

Word: “有的植物”  
Context: “有的植物吃了以后……”  
Alternatives: “一些植物” or “某些植物.” “有的植物” contrasts different kinds of plants. “一些植物” simply means “some plants,” while “某些植物” sounds more formal and emphasizes particular, unspecified types.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly expressed the important contrast between plants that cause pain and plants that make Shennong feel better, which gives your story a strong, logical flow.  
Next time, focus on introducing a character clearly at the beginning and using “了” for completed actions in a narrative.

## Scene 2

> 凉山很大的山，这也找植物。一天，他找到黄色的花。他听说你跟黄色的花做茶很有用。比方说，你的头发疼死，这冰茶是药。他一喝茶就很不舒服。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “凉山很大的山” / Formula: Place/name + 是 + 一座/一座很…的 + 山 / Correct: “凉山是一座很大的山。”

Incorrect phrase: “这也找植物” / Formula: Person + 在 + place + verb + object / Correct: “他也在这里找植物。”  
“这” refers to a place, so use “这里” (here), and the subject “他” needs to be stated.

Incorrect phrase: “你跟黄色的花做茶很有用” / Formula: 用 + ingredient/material + 做/泡 + object / Correct: “他听说用黄色的花泡茶很有用。”  
“跟” means “with” in the sense of “and/with someone”; use “用” for the material used to make tea. “你” is also unclear here.

Incorrect phrase: “比方说，你的头发疼死，这冰茶是药” / Formula: 比方说 + specific example; body part + 疼得 + degree / Correct: “比方说，头疼得厉害时，这种茶可以当药。”  
“头发” is hair; “头疼” means “to have a headache.” “比方说” should introduce a clear example of the tea being useful.

Incorrect phrase: “他一喝茶就很不舒服” / Formula: Subject + 一 + verb phrase + 就 + result / Correct: “可是，他一喝这种茶就觉得很不舒服。”  
The original pattern is good, but “这种茶” makes the reference clearer, and “觉得” sounds more natural with “不舒服.”

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “很大” / Context: “凉山是一座很大的山。” / Alternatives: “雄伟” describes a mountain as grand and majestic; “辽阔” emphasizes a wide, expansive area. “很大” is simple and neutral.

Word: “找” / Context: “他也在这里找植物。” / Alternatives: “寻找” is more formal and literary, while “采集” means to collect plants, herbs, or specimens rather than merely look for them.

Word: “黄色的花” / Context: “用黄色的花泡茶。” / Alternatives: “黄花” is shorter and more natural if you simply mean yellow flowers; “野花” emphasizes that the flowers grow in the wild.

Word: “很有用” / Context: “用黄色的花泡茶很有用。” / Alternatives: “有疗效” means that it has a real medicinal effect; “有益健康” means it is beneficial for one’s health. Both are more specific than “有用.”

Word: “很不舒服” / Context: “他一喝这种茶就觉得很不舒服。” / Alternatives: “身体不适” is more formal and can describe general physical discomfort; “感到恶心” specifically means feeling nauseous.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You created a clear little story with a setting, a useful flower tea, and an unexpected result at the end.  
Next time, focus on making pronoun references clear and using “用 + material + 做/泡 + object” accurately.

## Scene 3

> 糟糕，这黄色的花是夹竹桃，特别是毒。正好在这儿，灵芝药的夹竹桃他吃完了，也写一个课文的植物。谢谢神农。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “特别是毒” / Formula: 主语 + 特别 + 有 + 形容词 / Correct: “特别有毒。”  
“毒” is a noun; use “有毒” to describe something as poisonous.

Incorrect phrase: “正好在这儿，灵芝药的夹竹桃他吃完了” / Formula: 正好 + 地点 + 有 + 名词；谁 + 吃了 + 宾语 / Correct: “正好这儿有灵芝这种药。神农吃了夹竹桃。”  
“灵芝药的夹竹桃” incorrectly connects 灵芝 and 夹竹桃. 灵芝 is medicine, while 夹竹桃 is poisonous. Also, the subject “神农” should be stated clearly.

Incorrect phrase: “也写一个课文的植物” / Formula: 谁 + 也 + 写下了 + 宾语 + 的 + 用处 / Correct: “他也写下了这种植物的用处。”  
“写一个课文的植物” is not a natural phrase. We write down a plant’s “用处” (use/effect), not the plant itself.

Incorrect phrase: “谢谢神农。” / Formula: 谢谢 + 人 / Correct: “谢谢你，神农。” or “神农真厉害！”  
“谢谢神农” is understandable, but adding “你” sounds more like directly speaking to Shennong. “神农真厉害” fits better if you are narrating the story.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “糟糕” / Context: “糟糕，这黄色的花是夹竹桃。” / Alternatives: “不好了” or “危险！”  
“糟糕” expresses that something bad has happened and is natural here. “不好了” sounds more urgent, while “危险” directly emphasizes the danger of eating poisonous oleander.

Word: “黄色的花” / Context: “这黄色的花是夹竹桃。” / Alternatives: “黄花” or “黄色的夹竹桃”  
“黄色的花” is understandable but general. “黄花” is shorter in casual description. “黄色的夹竹桃” is more specific because it names the flower immediately.

Word: “特别” / Context: “特别有毒。” / Alternatives: “非常” or “剧毒”  
“特别有毒” is simple and clear. “非常有毒” is slightly more neutral. “剧毒” means “highly toxic” and is stronger and more precise.

Word: “灵芝药” / Context: “这儿有灵芝这种药。” / Alternatives: “灵芝” or “药材”  
“灵芝药” is not very natural. “灵芝” is sufficient when the reader knows it is medicinal. “药材” means medicinal ingredient or herb and sounds more advanced.

Word: “写” / Context: “他也写下了这种植物的用处。” / Alternatives: “记录” or “记载”  
“写下” means to write something down and is good for a simple story. “记录” means to record information and is more formal. “记载” is often used for recording facts in books or historical texts.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You clearly recognized the important contrast in the story: 夹竹桃 is poisonous, while 灵芝 is medicine.  
Next time, focus on making each sentence have a clear subject and keeping the actions in a logical order.
