## Scene 1

> 古代的时候，有一个人叫神农。一天，神农正在路上走，一看很多人正在吃一些植物。一些人一吃植物就生病了，神农想帮别人。所以想知道哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “古代的时候，有一个人叫神农。” / Formula: Time + 有一位/一个 + identity + 叫 + name / Correct: “在古代，有一位神农氏叫神农。”  
“神农氏” introduces him more clearly as a historical/legendary figure.

Incorrect phrase: “一天，神农正在路上走，一看很多人正在吃一些植物。” / Formula: Subject + 正在 + verb; 看到 + object / Correct: “一天，神农正在路上走，看到很多人正在吃各种植物。”  
“一看” usually means “as soon as one looks” or “at a glance”; use “看到” for “saw.”

Incorrect phrase: “一些人一吃植物就生病了” / Formula: Some people + 一 + verb phrase + 就 + result / Correct: “一些人一吃了某些植物就生病。”  
Use “某些植物” because it is not true that people get sick from eating every plant. “生病” can describe a repeated result here, so no final “了” is needed.

Incorrect phrase: “神农想帮别人。所以想知道……” / Formula: Subject + 想 + verb; therefore + subject + 想知道… / Correct: “神农想帮助人们，所以想知道哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃。”  
The second sentence has no subject, which makes the reference slightly unclear. Adding “神农” makes the meaning complete.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “人” / Context: “有一个人叫神农” / Alternatives: “人物” or “传说人物.” “人” is very general. “传说人物” is more precise and natural because Shennong is a legendary historical figure.

Word: “路上走” / Context: “神农正在路上走” / Alternatives: “赶路” or “行走.” “赶路” means travelling toward a destination and sounds more natural in a story. “行走” is more literary and descriptive.

Word: “一些植物” / Context: “很多人正在吃一些植物” / Alternatives: “各种植物” or “野生植物.” “一些” only means “some,” while “各种” emphasizes many different kinds. “野生植物” gives a clearer image of plants gathered in nature.

Word: “别人” / Context: “神农想帮别人” / Alternatives: “人们” or “百姓.” “别人” usually means “other people” in contrast to oneself. “人们” is more natural for people in general; “百姓” is especially suitable for people in an ancient-story setting.

Word: “不能吃” / Context: “哪些植物不能吃” / Alternatives: “有毒” or “不宜食用.” “不能吃” is simple and correct. “有毒” specifically means poisonous, while “不宜食用” means not suitable for eating and is more formal.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You created a clear cause-and-effect story: Shennong sees people get sick and wants to discover which plants are safe.  
Next time, focus on using “看到” rather than “一看,” and make sure each new sentence has a clear subject when needed.

## Scene 2

> 然后，神农开始尝很多植物，比方说他吃过生姜、芥兰和菠菜。一些植物吃真好，特别是黄瓜。但是，一些植物毒得很，特别是夹竹桃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “神农开始尝很多植物” / Formula: Subject + 开始 + 尝试/品尝 + (various kinds of) + noun / Correct: “神农开始尝试各种植物。”  
“尝” is usually followed by food or flavor, while “尝试” is more natural for testing many different plants.

Incorrect phrase: “一些植物吃真好” / Formula: Subject + 吃起来 + adverb + adjective / Correct: “一些植物吃起来真好。”  
Use “吃起来” to describe how food tastes or feels when eaten.

Incorrect phrase: “一些植物毒得很” / Formula: Subject + 很 + adjective; or Subject + 有毒 / Correct: “一些植物很有毒。”  
“毒得很” is understandable, but “很有毒” is the more standard and natural description for poisonous plants.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “比方说” / Context: “比方说他吃过生姜、芥兰和菠菜。” / Alternatives: “例如” is more concise and slightly more formal in writing. “比如” is very common and natural in both speech and writing. “比方说” is conversational and often introduces a longer example.

Word: “尝” / Context: “神农开始尝很多植物” / Alternatives: “尝试” means to test or experiment with something, so it fits Shennong’s careful testing of plants. “品尝” emphasizes tasting food for its flavor and enjoyment, so it is less suitable when he may be testing poisonous plants.

Word: “真好” / Context: “一些植物吃起来真好” / Alternatives: “很好吃” is the most natural everyday way to say that food tastes good. “美味” is more literary and emphasizes delicious flavor. “真好” is vague because it could describe many positive qualities, not specifically taste.

Word: “毒得很” / Context: “一些植物毒得很” / Alternatives: “有毒” is the standard factual description: poisonous. “剧毒” means extremely poisonous and creates a stronger, more dramatic tone. “毒得很” is understandable but less idiomatic here.

Word: “特别是” / Context: “特别是黄瓜／夹竹桃” / Alternatives: “其中” means “among them” and is useful for neutrally identifying an example: “其中，黄瓜很好吃。” “尤其是” emphasizes that one item stands out more strongly than the others and is slightly more formal than “特别是.”

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You used a clear contrast between beneficial plants and poisonous plants, and your examples make the scene easy to understand.  
Next time, focus on using result or experience complements such as “吃起来很好吃” and more precise verbs such as “尝试.”

## Scene 3

> 一个人对神农说，麻烦我们可以吃什么植物？神农说，这就要看你想不想生病了。以后，神农告诉别人一些植物能吃，一些植物不能吃。

**Grammar Corrections**

Incorrect phrase: “麻烦我们可以吃什么植物？” / Formula: 请问 + question / Correct: “请问，我们可以吃什么植物？”

Incorrect phrase: “这就要看你想不想生病了。” / Formula: Question/uncertain result + 这就要看 + A-not-A question + 了 / Correct: “什么植物能吃，这就要看植物有没有毒了。”  
“这就要看……” should explain an uncertain result. Here, whether someone *wants* to get sick does not determine which plants are edible.

Incorrect phrase: “以后，神农告诉别人一些植物能吃，一些植物不能吃。” / Formula: 后来/之后 + subject + 告诉 + person + content / Correct: “后来，神农告诉人们，哪些植物能吃，哪些植物不能吃。”  
“以后” usually means “in the future”; “后来” is better for the next event in a past story. “哪些……，哪些……” is also clearer here.

**Vocabulary Suggestions**

Word: “一个人” / Context: “一个人对神农说” / Alternatives: “一个农民” or “一位村民.” “一个人” is very general and vague; “农民” or “村民” gives the speaker a clearer identity and fits a story about plants.

Word: “麻烦” / Context: “麻烦我们可以吃什么植物？” / Alternatives: “请问” or “请教一下.” “麻烦” can mean “trouble” or “to inconvenience someone.” “请问” is the standard polite expression for asking a question, while “请教一下” sounds respectful and emphasizes asking for someone’s knowledge.

Word: “吃什么植物” / Context: “我们可以吃什么植物？” / Alternatives: “哪些植物可以食用” or “哪些植物能吃.” “能吃” is natural and simple. “可以食用” is more formal and precise, especially when discussing edible plants.

Word: “告诉别人” / Context: “神农告诉别人” / Alternatives: “告诉人们” or “教人们分辨.” “别人” means other people but is less specific here. “人们” fits a general audience, while “教人们分辨” means “teach people to distinguish,” which better describes teaching edible versus poisonous plants.

Word: “不能吃” / Context: “一些植物不能吃” / Alternatives: “有毒” or “不能食用.” “不能吃” is a basic expression. “有毒” clearly explains the danger, while “不能食用” is a more formal way to say a plant is not edible.

**Encouragement and Next Steps**

You used a clear dialogue format and correctly showed that Shennong teaches people which plants are safe to eat.  
Next time, focus on using “这就要看……” to describe a real uncertain condition, such as whether a plant is poisonous.
