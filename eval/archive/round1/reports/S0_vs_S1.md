> 他发现这个口味，一百岁时候，不小心吃了毒的花。一吃就开始死。但是生病时候告诉人什么植物是药，什么植物是毒。

#### S0 (before)

> **Grammar Corrections**
> 
> Incorrect phrase: “他发现这个口味” / Formula: 谁 + 尝了/吃了 + 某种食物 + ，发现 + 它的味道/它是…… / Correct: “他尝了这种花，发现它的味道很特别。”  
> “口味” usually refers to a person’s food preference (e.g. 我的口味比较清淡). For the taste of a food or plant, use “味道.”
> 
> Incorrect phrase: “一百岁时候” / Formula: 年龄 + 的时候 / Correct: “他一百岁的时候”  
> Add “的” after an age expression.
> 
> Incorrect phrase: “吃了毒的花” / Formula: 吃了 + 有毒的 + 名词 / Correct: “吃了有毒的花”  
> “有毒的花” is the natural way to say “a poisonous flower.”
> 
> Incorrect phrase: “一吃就开始死” / Formula: 一 + 动作 + 就 + 结果 / Correct: “一吃下去就中毒了。”  
> “开始死” is not natural in Chinese. “中毒了” clearly describes what happens after eating a poisonous plant. If he actually died, say “一吃下去就死了.”
> 
> Incorrect phrase: “但是生病时候告诉人什么植物是药，什么植物是毒” / Formula: 谁 + 在/……的时候 + 告诉 + 人们 + 哪些……，哪些…… / Correct: “但是，他生病的时候还是告诉人们哪些植物是药，哪些植物有毒。”  
> Use “在……的时候” or “……的时候,” add the subject “他,” and use “人们” rather than just “人.” “哪些” is more natural because you are talking about multiple plants.
> 
> **Vocabulary Suggestions**
> 
> Word: “口味” / Context: “他发现这个口味” / Alternatives: “味道” means the taste or flavor of a particular food, flower, or plant. “口味” means someone’s personal preference in food, such as liking spicy or light food.
> 
> Word: “毒的花” / Context: “吃了毒的花” / Alternatives: “有毒的花” is the standard, neutral expression for a poisonous flower. “毒花” is shorter and can sound more literary or descriptive, but “有毒的花” is clearer for a beginner.
> 
> Word: “开始死” / Context: “一吃就开始死” / Alternatives: “中毒” means to be poisoned and is the best choice here. “昏倒” means to faint, while “死亡” is a more formal word for death. These choices make the result more precise.
> 
> Word: “告诉人” / Context: “告诉人什么植物是药” / Alternatives: “告诉人们” is more natural when speaking to people in general. “提醒人们” means to warn or remind people, especially about danger, so it fits well when talking about poisonous plants.
> 
> Word: “药” / Context: “什么植物是药” / Alternatives: “药材” refers specifically to medicinal materials, often natural plants used in Chinese medicine. “草药” means herbal medicine and is especially suitable when discussing healing plants.
> 
> **Encouragement and Next Steps**
> 
> You did well to use “一……就……” to show a quick result after eating the flower.  
> Next time, focus on adding “有” in descriptions such as “有毒的植物” and using clear subjects in every sentence.
